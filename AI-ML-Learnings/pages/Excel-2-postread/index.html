Before The Formulas

00 AI in Sheets — Gemini

Gemini lives inside Google Sheets — no separate tab, no separate app. It reads your data and executes plain-English instructions. It's fast at "doing." It is not a substitute for "thinking" or for verifying its own output.

What Gemini can and can't do
  • ✅ Clean/standardize data, remove duplicates, fix formatting, write formulas, summarize a sheet
  • ❌ Make the business decision for you, or verify its own errors — that stays your job

The responsible-analyst rule: spot-check ~5 rows before/after, check the row count didn't silently shrink, never run it on your only copy of the raw data, and remember — your name goes on the report, not Gemini's.

Foundations

01 Cell referencing

Dragging a formula down asks one question per reference: "should this move too?" Pick a reference style below and watch what row 2's formula becomes by row 5.

Think of GSheets as a copy machine. When you drag a formula down, it doesn't just duplicate the text — for every reference inside it, it asks "should I move one row down too?" A plain reference like A1 says yes, so =A1+B1 in row 2 becomes =A2+B2 in row 3, =A3+B3 in row 4, and so on. The small blue square at the bottom-right of a selected cell is the Fill Handle — dragging it copies the formula while adjusting references based on which type they are. Double-clicking the Fill Handle (instead of dragging it) auto-fills all the way down to the last row that has adjacent data, which is faster for large datasets.

Try it: build your own reference

Column to divide/multiply by row (relative), and a fixed cell to lock. Toggle the $ signs and see the row-3 and row-4 result.

03 Basic aggregation

Before any deep analysis, an analyst checks the scale of the data — total revenue, count of orders, highest value. These "pulse-check" formulas take seconds but give critical context. SUM, COUNT, COUNTA, MAX, MIN, AVERAGE — computed live from the Sales column of your dataset above.

COUNT only counts numeric cells. COUNTA counts everything non-empty — use it for text columns like Order ID.

String Building

04 Joining text with &

In advertising, logistics, and reporting, you often need to merge data into a unique key or a readable label. The & operator is the fastest way to join text in GSheets — the literal string in the middle (a space, dash, comma, or nothing at all) becomes your separator. Type any two values and a separator below — see the joined "Shipping Label" the way =A2 & " - " & F2 would build it.

Same result either way: =A2 & " - " & F2 or =CONCATENATE(A2," - ",F2)

Conditional Logic

05 IF — the yes/no machine

One condition, one fork in the road. In SQL you'd reach for CASE WHEN Sales > 500 THEN 'Large Order' ELSE 'Standard' END — IF is GSheets' version of exactly that. Test your own Sales value against the Large Order threshold.

SYNTAX
=IF( condition , value_if_true , value_if_false )
DomainReal-world use case
E-commerceFlag orders above a threshold for same-day shipping
FinanceMark transactions above a limit for compliance review
HRClassify employees as "Senior" or "Junior" based on years of experience
InventoryFlag items with stock < 10 as "Reorder Required"

06 IFS — multi-bucket logic

Real business conditions are rarely binary — "is this a retail order, a small batch, or a bulk order?" is three buckets in one column, which nested IFs handle clumsily. IFS checks conditions left to right, returns the first match, and mirrors SQL's multiple WHEN clauses inside one CASE. TRUE as the last condition acts as the catch-all, like SQL's ELSE.

SYNTAX
=IFS( cond1, val1, cond2, val2, ... , TRUE, fallback )
DomainReal-world use case
LogisticsClassify shipments into Parcel / Pallet / Container based on weight
SalesCategorize customers as Bronze / Silver / Gold / Platinum by annual spend
EducationAssign letter grades (A/B/C/D/F) from numeric scores
BankingTier loan applicants by credit score range
Order matters: IFS evaluates left to right and stops at the first TRUE condition — arrange conditions from most specific to least specific, or a broader condition earlier in the list can capture values before a more specific one ever gets checked.
Logical Functions

07 AND vs. OR

Some business conditions depend on two things happening simultaneously (AND) or at least one of several things being true (OR) — almost always combined with IF. In SQL you'd write WHERE Region = 'South' AND Sales < 100; in GSheets that becomes =IF(AND(F2="South", I2<100), "Flag", "Clear"). AND is a vault door — every lock must open. OR is a keypad — any one key works. Flip the two switches below and watch both results update.

=AND(cond1, cond2)

Returns TRUE only if both are TRUE.

=OR(cond1, cond2)

Returns TRUE if at least one is TRUE.

Applied to your dataset

Inspection Status (AND) and Priority Status (OR) in the live table above are computed with exactly this logic — edit Region/Sales/Order Type there and come back to see the gate react.

AND — real-world use case
Risk management: flag transactions that are both high-value AND from a new account
HR: identify employees due for promotion (tenure > 3 years AND performance = "Excellent")
Compliance: flag orders that are large AND from a restricted region
OR — real-world use case
E-commerce: notify customers if an order is delayed OR the item is out of stock
Support: escalate tickets that are high-priority OR unresolved for > 48 hours
Sales: identify a lead as "Hot" if they visited the site OR opened the email
Arithmetic Aggregation

08 SUMIF · COUNTIF · SUMIFS · COUNTIFS

In SQL: SELECT SUM(Sales) FROM superstore WHERE Region = 'South'. In GSheets: =SUMIF(F2:F20, "South", I2:I20) — a targeted calculator that sums a range only where a condition is met. Build a query against your live dataset — one condition or two — and see the exact formula plus the live answer.

Single condition

=SUMIF(F2:F,region,I2:I)
=COUNTIF(F2:F,region)

Two conditions — the "S" versions

=SUMIFS(I2:I,F2:F,region,J2:J,op&val)
=COUNTIFS(F2:F,region,J2:J,op&val)
Watch the order: in SUMIF the sum range is LAST. In SUMIFS it moves to FIRST. Mixing these up is the single most common formula bug in this lecture.
FunctionReal-world use case
SUMIFTotal revenue per category, per region, or per salesperson
COUNTIFCount how many orders are classified as "Retail" or "Bulk"
SUMIFSRevenue from a specific region AND product category at the same time
COUNTIFSCount high-value orders from a specific region AND time period

Check Yourself

11 Quick quiz

1. You stored a discount rate in K1 and want Discounted Sales for all rows using Sales from column I. Which formula correctly uses an absolute reference?
I2 should change row-by-row (relative), while $K$1 must stay fixed because the discount rate lives in one constant cell.
2. What is the difference between AND and OR functions in Google Sheets?
AND() needs every condition to pass. OR() needs just one matching condition.
3. You want total Sales only for the West region AND Quantity >= 5. Which function is most appropriate?
SUMIFS() is used when the aggregation depends on two or more conditions at once.
4. What is the difference between COUNT and COUNTA?
COUNT only looks at numeric cells. COUNTA counts every non-empty cell, text included.
