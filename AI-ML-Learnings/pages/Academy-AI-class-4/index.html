Section 1 · 5 minThe amnesia problem you already solved once

You have shipped an app. Its instructions live in summarizer.py, in a variable called SYSTEM_PROMPT, and your code sends them on every single call without you thinking about it.

So you have already solved this problem once, in code.

Now open a chat window and the model has never heard of you. You paste your instructions again. And again. Every new conversation.

Ask LearnersHow many of you have a prompt saved in a notes app that you paste regularly? What is it for?
Note for InstructorTake two answers. Most rooms have at least one person pasting the same block daily. Point out that their own app does not have this problem, because the prompt is in the file, and that is exactly what today's tools do for the chat window.

Every major platform has shipped the same answer: a place to save your instructions so they are applied automatically. Today you set that up on all three, and then we talk about what happened to two of them this month.

Section 2 · 9 minThree ways to make a model remember

When people say they want AI that "knows our business", they mean one of three different things. They get muddled constantly and they have different answers.

What you want What it is called What it actually does
It should always behave this way Saved instructions Your system prompt, stored and re-sent for you
It should know these documents Attached knowledge Your files sit alongside the chat and the model reads from them
It should be able to do things Connected tools The model can call something outside itself

Today is the first two. The third is the second half of this module.

What none of them are

ImportantNone of these train the model. Nothing you type into a skill or a project changes the model itself. Your instructions and your files are supplied to the model at the moment you ask, exactly as if you had pasted them. The platform is doing the pasting.

That is worth saying twice, because people assume otherwise and then trust the result too much. It is automated pasting, not learning. You know this already: SYSTEM_PROMPT in your own file is not training anything either.

Doubt by LearnerSo why does my assistant seem to get better the more I use it?

Usually because you refined the instructions, not because it learned. Some platforms do have a separate memory feature that carries facts between chats, and that is a different thing. If you did not edit the instructions and it still improved, memory is the likely explanation, or your own prompts got better.

Section 3 · 15 minGemini Skills, hands-on

Note for InstructorHands-on, 15 minutes. Everyone builds alongside you. Skills are on personal Google accounts for users aged 18 and over. The click path below was verified on the live interface; if it has moved by the time you teach, the fast-changing block in Section 9 is where to record the correction.

A skill is a saved set of instructions that Gemini applies when it is relevant, or when you call it by name. That is the whole idea.

1
Go to https://gemini.google.com and find Customise in the left sidebar. If you do not see it, click More first.
2
On the Customise page, open the Skills tab. You will see Discover, Connectors and Skills across the top.
3
Click Create at the top right. You get three options: Create with Gemini, Create manually, and Upload. Choose Create manually.
4
Fill in the three fields.
Field What goes in it
Name Lowercase words joined by hyphens, as the placeholder shows: release-notes-writer
Description One line on when this skill should be used
Instructions The actual rules, the same shape as your SYSTEM_PROMPT
5
Paste this into Instructions.
You turn rough developer notes into release notes that customers can read.

For every set of notes you receive:
- Lead with what changed for the customer, not what changed in the code
- Group items under: New, Improved, Fixed
- One sentence per item, in plain language
- No ticket numbers, branch names, internal service names or acronyms
- If an item only matters to the team and not to a customer, leave it out

If the notes are too vague to write an entry, list that item under
"Need more detail" instead of guessing what it does.
6
Click Create at the top right.
7
Start a new chat and type / followed by the skill name. Then paste these notes.
- bumped auth-svc to 2.4.1, fixes the token refresh race (JIRA-8821)
- added dark mode toggle in settings
- cart no longer loses items when you switch tabs
- refactored the payment retry loop, no user visible change
- new export button on reports page, csv only for now
- fixed that thing where the date picker shows wrong month in Safari
8
Check three things in the output: did it drop the refactor, did it drop JIRA-8821, and did it group correctly.
Ask LearnersDid yours drop the refactor line? Yes or no in the chat.
Note for InstructorExpect a split, and that is useful rather than a problem: the same instructions do not produce identical behaviour every run. If most kept the refactor, improve the rule together. Change "leave it out" to "leave it out, including anything described as a refactor, cleanup or internal change", and re-run. That is Class 2's iteration loop applied to a saved prompt.
ImportantTwo things that differ from a Gem. You can hold up to 100 active skills, and you can combine several in one chat. But most of the old Gem tools do not work inside a skill, including Deep Research, Canvas and Guided Learning. If you need Deep Research, you run it in a normal chat, which is Section 8.
Doubt by LearnerDo I have to type the slash every time?

No. Gemini can also apply a skill on its own when it judges it relevant, which is why the Description field matters. The slash is how you force it.

Section 4 · 15 minChatGPT: the global layer and Projects, hands-on

Note for InstructorHands-on, 15 minutes. ChatGPT is the platform most learners already use. Projects are available on every plan including Free.

ChatGPT splits the same idea across two features, and knowing which is which saves a lot of confusion.

Feature Scope Use it for
Custom instructions Every chat you have, everywhere How you want to be spoken to, always
Projects Only chats inside that project A specific job, with its own instructions and files

Custom instructions: the global layer

1
Open Settings, then Personalization. Scroll to Custom instructions and open the ChatGPT row, described as "Give ChatGPT extra instructions and context for all chats".
2
Put something genuinely yours in it. For example: I am a backend developer in India. Use metric units and rupees. Give me the answer first and the explanation after. Do not open replies with a compliment.
3
Start any new chat and ask anything. The change applies everywhere, with no project involved.
Note for InstructorThe same Personalization page now also has preset dials above custom instructions: Base style and tone, Warmth, Enthusiasm, Emoji, Visual answers, and Headers and lists. Point them out in one line. They are the same idea as a written instruction, pre-packaged, and they are a good illustration that a preference and a job belong in different places.
ImportantThis is the right slot for preferences and the wrong slot for a job. Anything task-specific belongs in a project, otherwise it leaks into every unrelated conversation you have.

Projects: the same assistant you just built

1
In the left sidebar, find the Projects section and click the add new project control next to it.
2
Name it "Release Notes".
3
Open the project's instructions and paste the same instruction block from Section 3. The same text. That is the point.
4
Start a chat inside the project, paste the same developer notes, and compare the output to what your Gemini skill produced.
Ask LearnersWhich of the two gave you the more usable release notes? There is no correct answer and the disagreement is the interesting part.
Note for InstructorTake two or three answers and resist settling it. Class 1 said the best model is the one that answers your question best. This is that lesson with evidence the learners generated themselves.
Doubt by LearnerIf I put instructions in both custom instructions and a project, which wins?

Both are applied. They stack rather than compete, which is fine when they agree and confusing when they contradict. Keep preferences global and jobs local and you will not hit it.

Section 5 · 9 minClaude Projects

Claude's version combines both slots in one place: instructions and files, scoped to a project.

1
Go to https://claude.ai/projects and click + New Project at the top right.
2
Give it a name and a description. Worth knowing: Claude cannot see either of those fields. They are for you.
3
On the project page, click Set project instructions, paste the same block again, and click Save instructions.
4
Add knowledge with the + button in the project knowledge area on the right.
5
Run the same notes through it.

You have now set up the same assistant three times. The instructions did not change once. Only the box they went into changed.

ImportantThat portability is the real takeaway, and Section 9 is why it is not a theoretical concern.
Note for InstructorFree accounts can keep up to five projects, so if a learner hits a limit, that is why. On paid plans a project with a lot of attached material switches to a retrieval mode rather than putting everything in context. Name that and move on; it is Class 5's topic.

Section 6 · 11 minWhich one when

The honest answer is that for saved instructions all three do the same job, and you should use whichever platform you already live in.

Gemini skill ChatGPT Project Claude Project
Saved instructions Yes Yes Yes
Attached files Yes Yes Yes
Separate chat history for the job No Yes Yes
A global "always do this" layer Partly, via auto-applied skills Yes, custom instructions No, per project
Invoke it by name mid-chat Yes, with / No, you open the project No, you open the project
Free tier can build it Yes, 18 and over Yes Yes, up to five projects

The actual decision

Pick by the shape of your work, not the feature list.

If you want something you call on mid-conversation, a skill fits. It is the only one of the three you can summon by name without leaving the chat you are in.

If the work has an ongoing thread, where you want the chats about this one job kept together, use a project. That is the real difference: a project is a workspace with a history.

If you want a rule that applies to everything you ever ask, that is ChatGPT's custom instructions.

Ask LearnersName one task of yours and say which of the three you would use. One line.

Section 7 · 10 minAttaching knowledge: what a file does and does not do

This is where expectations get set badly, so be precise.

Attaching a file means the model can use its contents when it answers. It is the grounding idea from Class 3, made permanent for that space instead of pasted per chat.

What it does

It answers from your material rather than from memory. Upload your actual refund policy and the answers come from that policy, not from a general sense of what refund policies usually say.

It survives across chats in that space. You upload once, not every conversation.

What it does not do

It does not train the model. Same point as Section 2, and worth repeating because this is where people believe it hardest.

It does not guarantee the model reads all of it. With a lot of material, platforms retrieve the parts that look relevant rather than supplying everything. A fact that exists in your file can still be missed.

It does not make the answers true. An out-of-date document gives you a confident assistant citing an out-of-date document.

Note for InstructorLive demo, 4 minutes, and it does not depend on the model failing. Ask the Release Notes project a question your notes do not cover, such as what the refund window is. Then upload any short policy document and ask again. The change from a general answer to a specific sourced one is immediate and reproduces every batch.
ImportantThe Class 3 rule still applies and matters more here, not less. Add "answer only from the attached files, and say so if it is not in them" to your instructions. Attaching a file does not stop the model falling back on general knowledge unless you say so.

Section 8 · 13 minDeep Research

All three platforms have a research mode, and it is one of the most used features in any of them.

What it actually is

A normal answer takes seconds and comes from one pass. Research mode takes several minutes. The model plans what to look into, runs many searches, reads what it finds, and writes a structured report with citations.

It is the first thing in this module that visibly thinks and acts in a loop before answering. Hold that thought, because that loop is what Class 7 calls an agent.

When it is worth it

Use research mode Use a normal question
You would otherwise open fifteen tabs One fact you can check in one tab
Comparing options across many sources Something in a document you already have
You need the sources listed for someone else You just need a quick answer for yourself
The answer should survive being forwarded Speed matters more than depth
Note for InstructorLive demo, 6 minutes, and start it early. Launch the research run at the beginning of the section, teach the rest while it works, then come back to the finished report. Do not watch a progress bar with thirty people.
1
Pick a question with real scope. For example: Compare the three main electric two-wheeler brands available in Pune on price, range, service network and real owner complaints. Cite your sources.
2
Launch it in whichever platform you have a free run available on, then carry on teaching.
3
When it finishes, scroll the report and show the citation list. Then open two of the citations.
ImportantEverything from Class 3 applies. A research report is far better grounded than a plain answer, because the model is quoting things it actually fetched. It is not verified. The citations are the point, and the point is only worth anything if somebody clicks them.
ImportantOn Gemini, Deep Research does not run inside a skill. It is one of the tools that did not carry over. Run it in an ordinary chat.

Writing a brief that gets a good report

Research mode rewards the same specificity as any prompt, plus two things of its own. Say what the comparison axes are, so the report has a spine. Say what the output is for, so the depth and tone fit.

Ask LearnersGive me a question from your own work that would genuinely be worth a research run rather than a normal question.
Doubt by LearnerDoes it search the whole internet?

It searches what its search tool can reach, which is a lot but not everything, and it cannot see anything behind a login or a paywall. For regulated or academic work, treat the report as a starting map, not a literature review.

Section 9 · 7 minThe month both vendors changed their minds

Note for Instructor7 minutes, and this is the most valuable part of the class. Do not rush it to finish early.

You set up the same assistant three times today and the instructions never changed. Here is why that mattered.

What happened

Gemini Gems OpenAI Custom GPTs
Announced Replaced by skills Retiring, announced 11 September 2026
Can you still make one No, read-only from 17 November 2026 No, creation restricted to Business, Enterprise and Edu
When it stops Removed 17 December 2026 Stops running 11 December 2026
What replaces it Skills Plugins, with the instructions becoming skills
Workspace or business accounts March 2027, education June 2027 Follows each workspace's own notice

Two different companies, the same six weeks, the same direction. Both moved from "build a custom assistant object" to "save reusable instructions that the model picks up when relevant", and both called the new thing skills.

The lesson, which is the actual content of this section

ImportantThe valuable thing is your instructions and your files. The feature wrapping them is temporary, and this month is the proof. Keep the text of your instructions somewhere you own, in a document or a repository, and treat every platform's box as a place you paste them into.

Your own app already does this correctly. SYSTEM_PROMPT sits in a file you control, and no vendor can retire it.

Ask LearnersWhere do your prompts live right now? If the honest answer is "inside one vendor's product", that is today's action item.
Note for InstructorAdvanced learner extension, if the room is ahead: ask what they would do differently if they had built a product on Custom GPTs in June. The answer people reach is an export, a version history, and a thin layer of their own between the vendor and the user. That is the right instinct and it is worth naming.

Section 10 · 4 minWrap-up and what is next

What you can do now

You can name the three things people mean by "AI that knows our business", and say which of them saved instructions actually solves.

You can set up a reusable assistant on all three major platforms, and you know the difference that matters: a skill is summoned by name mid-chat, a project is a workspace with a history, custom instructions are the global layer.

You can attach knowledge to a space and say honestly what that does and does not buy you.

You can run a research brief and you know its value is in the citations, which means clicking them.

And you know to keep your instructions somewhere you own, because you watched two vendors retire theirs in the same month.
