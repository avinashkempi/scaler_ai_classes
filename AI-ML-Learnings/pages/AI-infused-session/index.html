2.3 · What is an LLM?

Large Language Model (LLM)

An AI system trained on massive amounts of text to predict the next word (or "token") in a sequence. You give it text, it continues the text. That's the entire core idea.

⌨️
Analogy · Phone Autocomplete on Steroids

Type "The capital of France is…" and the LLM predicts "Paris" — because it has seen this pattern millions of times in training. It's your phone's keyboard suggestion, except it has read the internet. (Full simulator coming in Section 5 — this is just the trailer.)

Why "Large"?

Billions of internal parameters — think of them as billions of tiny tuning knobs on a mixing board, each adjusted during training.

Trained on what?

Internet-scale text: books, websites, code, articles. "Large" data + "large" knobs = handles a huge range of tasks.

Multimodal?

Modern LLMs also process images, audio, video. But the core engine is still the same: predict the next token.

2.5 · AI Agent vs Agentic AI — finally, clearly

🎙 Instructor Says

"These two terms confuse everyone — including people who work in AI. Here's the clean version:"

"An AI Agent is a thing — one worker. One system with an LLM brain, some tools, some memory, doing one job: a refund agent, a flight-booking agent, a coding agent."

"Agentic AI is a way of working — the paradigm. AI that plans a goal into steps, acts using tools, checks its own results, and retries when something fails. Very often it shows up as a team of agents coordinated by a manager agent."

"Simplest mental model: an AI Agent is an employee. Agentic AI is how a whole company operates. One employee ≠ a company — a company has planning, delegation, collaboration and quality checks. That's the difference."

🙋 Ask Learners

When we talk about the planning-acting-checking style, or teams of agents, we say 'agentic'."

🧑‍🍳
Analogy · One Cook vs a Kitchen

An AI Agent is one cook who makes great biryani. Agentic AI is the whole restaurant kitchen — a head chef planning the menu, station cooks executing, a taster rejecting bad plates and sending them back. Same skill inside, completely different level of organisation.

🚗
Analogy · Paradigm vs Instance

"Electric mobility" is a paradigm; a Tata Nexon EV is one specific instance of it. Agentic AI : AI Agent :: Electric mobility : Nexon EV. (Same as OOP the paradigm vs one Java class you write.)

PART B · § 5

How LLMs Actually Work — The Autocomplete Secret

⏱ 12 min

Forget the movies. No sentience, no Skynet. Here is the single most honest explanation of an LLM you will ever hear — and then you'll operate one yourself.

🎙 Instructor Says

"You know how your phone predicts the next word when you're texting? 'Hey, want to grab' — it suggests lunch, coffee, dinner. Now imagine your phone had read every book ever written. Every Wikipedia article. Every Stack Overflow answer. Every GitHub repo. Billions of pages."

"And instead of predicting one word, it could keep going — entire paragraphs, essays, code. That's an LLM. It's a next-word prediction engine trained on an extraordinary amount of text. It doesn't 'understand' the way you do. It doesn't 'think'. It predicts what text should come next — and because it has read SO much, it's shockingly good at it."

🔮 Be the LLM — predict the next token

Pick a starting sentence. The bars show the probability of each candidate next word — exactly what an LLM computes billions of times per answer. Click a word to append it and watch the sentence build itself.

▌

👆 Higher bar = more likely. The model doesn't "choose the truth" — it chooses the statistically most likely continuation of everything it has read. This one insight explains prompt engineering, hallucinations, and the wider AI workflow.

🎭
Analogy · The Brilliant Mimic

Think of an LLM as a mimic who has memorized every book in the library and can reproduce & remix patterns incredibly well — even though it doesn't "understand" the way a human does. The better your input text, the better its prediction of what comes next — which is exactly why Prompt Engineering deserves a dedicated session.

Token

A chunk of text the LLM reads and predicts, step by step — sometimes a whole word, sometimes part of a word, sometimes punctuation. "programming" might be two tokens: program + ming. LLMs see text as LEGO bricks, not words. (Full playground in Section 7.)

Quiz 3 · At its core, what does a Large Language Model actually do?

AIt searches the internet in real-time to find answers
BIt predicts the most likely next token based on all the text it was trained on
CIt stores a copy of every book and website and retrieves relevant passages
DIt thinks through problems logically, like a human brain
Why B? This is one of the most important concepts in this session. An LLM doesn't search — it predicts. It doesn't think — it predicts. It doesn't know — it has learned patterns and predicts what text should come next. Internalize this, and prompt engineering, hallucinations, and context limits all click into place.

Closed vs Open Source — why it matters

🔒 Closed (GPT, Claude, Gemini)

  • Runs on the company's servers — you send data, get results back
  • Like Gmail: convenient & powerful, but Google holds your emails
  • Zero infrastructure work; pay per use

🔓 Open (Llama, Mistral)

  • Download & run on your own machines — full control
  • Like running your own email server: more control, more work
  • Data never leaves your infrastructure

Advanced note (plant the seed): production teams often use a "router" pattern — simple tasks go to smaller, cheaper models; hard tasks go to frontier models. A load balancer, but for intelligence.

The Three Dials — Tokens, Context, Temperature

⏱ 17 min

Every LLM — regardless of brand — exposes the same three concepts. Master these dials and you can walk into any AI tool and know exactly what's happening.

Dial A · Tokens — the atoms of AI language

🧱
Analogy · LEGO Bricks + Jio Data Pack

LLMs don't read words — they read tokens, LEGO-sized chunks of text. And tokens are your data pack: every prompt consumes allocation, every API call bills per token. Wasteful tokens = burning money and hitting limits.

SituationWhy tokens matter
API billingYou pay per token — input AND output
Context limitsThe model can only handle X tokens total
Response lengthYou can cap output tokens to control cost
SpeedMore tokens = slower response
Practical note: never count tokens manually. Just remember — shorter, clearer prompts = fewer tokens = cheaper and faster.

Dial B · Context Window — the model's desk

🪑
Analogy · Desk Size

The context window is the model's desk. Everything it can "see" — your prompt, the whole conversation, pasted documents, AND its reply — must fit on the desk. When you run out, papers fall off the edge: the model literally forgets the start of your chat. Ever had a long ChatGPT chat contradict itself from 30 messages ago? Desk got full.

Dial C · Temperature — the creativity dial

👨‍🍳
Analogy · Recipe Chef vs Jazz Chef

Temperature 0 = a chef following the recipe exactly. Same dish every time — perfect for code and SQL. Temperature 1 = a jazz chef improvising. "Let's throw in sriracha and see." More creative, more varied… occasionally inedible.

TemperatureUse caseWhy
0.0Code generation, bug fixes, SQLYou want THE correct answer, every time
0.1 – 0.3Technical docs, test cases, refactoringMostly precise, tiny natural variation

🧑‍💻
GitHub Copilot

Runs COLD (low temp)

Code autocomplete must be predictable. You want correctness, not a creative interpretation of correctness.

📣
Marketing copy tools

Run HOT (high temp)

Ad-copy generators like Jasper run warmer — variety IS the product when you need ten different taglines.

Practical note: chat apps (ChatGPT, Claude.ai) pick a sensible default for you. You'll control temperature yourself when calling APIs in hands-on workflows.
Heads up: this simulator's dial runs 0–1 to keep things simple, but some model APIs (e.g. OpenAI's) actually accept temperature 0 to 2 — the scale isn't identical across providers, so always check the docs for the model you're calling.
For the curious — two cousins of temperature: Top-K lets the model pick only from the K most likely next tokens (Top-K = 5 → only the top 5 candidates). Top-p (nucleus sampling) picks from the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities add up to p. Both are just different ways of trimming the candidate list.
🙋 Ask Learners

"If you were building a loan eligibility checker for an NBFC, what temperature would you set — and why? Drop your answer in chat."

(Expected: near 0 — a loan decision must be consistent and defensible. Two identical applicants must get identical answers. Great moment to connect temperature to real regulatory stakes.)

Quiz 4 · You're building an AI code-review tool that must give consistent, deterministic feedback every time. Which temperature?

A0.9 — for creative and diverse feedback
B0.5 — for a nice balance
C0.0 — for deterministic, reliable results
DTemperature doesn't matter for code review
Why C? You want the same bugs flagged every single time. Imagine a reviewer whose feedback changed with their mood — that's high temperature. Low temperature = the model always picks the most probable token = exactly right for factual, code-related, deterministic tasks. Save the heat for brainstorming.

Wrap-Up — Your AI Toolkit Mental Model

⏱ 10 min

Two hours ago, AI was a fog of buzzwords. Here's the map you now carry.

1 · GenAI = the world's most well-read autocomplete

LLMs predict what text comes next. That's it. But trained on enough data, a simple rule produces something extraordinary — like "buy low, sell high" underpins all of finance.

2 · The Big 5 each have a personality

Swiss Army Knife (GPT), Thoughtful Senior Dev (Claude), The Elephant (Gemini), The Home Lab (Llama), The Euro Specialist (Mistral).

3 · Three dials control everything

Tokens — the currency. Context Window — the desk size. Temperature — the creativity dial.

4 · No single model is "best"

The best model depends on the task. Test important work across models — like benchmarking databases before choosing one for production.

5 · The skill that matters is USING AI well

You don't need to understand transformers. You need to understand how to get great output from these tools — which is a core skill this session helps build.
