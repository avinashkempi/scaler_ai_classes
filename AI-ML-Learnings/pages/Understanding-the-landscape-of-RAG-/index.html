Understanding the Landscape of RAG

AI assistants can now be given rules and a permanent memory — and a whole folder of files to work from. But having forty files uploaded isn't the same as the assistant using the right five when you ask a question. How does an AI actually decide which of those files — and which passages inside them — to read before it answers? That mechanism is called RAG. Today, we open that hood and tour the landscape: what RAG is, where it fails, and the three no-code tools — Gemini Notebook, Perplexity and NoteGPT — that put it to work.

Modern AI assistants can be given rules (a system prompt) and persistent memory (a Gemini Gem, a Claude Project, an AI Studio playground), so nobody has to re-brief them every conversation. So what can still go wrong? The assistant can keep following its rules, still cite a source exactly the way it's supposed to — and cite the wrong one: an old report, while four newer, more relevant ones sit untouched in the same Project.

Meet The Retrieval Problem

That's not a memory problem. That's a retrieval problem — and it's been hiding in plain sight since the moment the first file was uploaded. Picture a brilliant analyst with perfect memory — they remember their job flawlessly. But hand them a filing cabinet with forty folders, and ask a question that spans five of them, and there's no guarantee they open the right five. They might grab the first folder their hand lands on and call it a day. Confidently cited, still wrong — arguably more dangerous than an honest guess, because it looks verified.

Quiz 1 · A user's query to an AI assistant pulled one stale workload report instead of all five relevant ones. Which step of RAG failed?

AGenerate — the model wrote a poorly-worded answer
BRetrieve — the system searched the knowledge base and returned an incomplete set of relevant chunks
CAugment — the retrieved chunks were formatted incorrectly before being added to the prompt
DThe system prompt was missing a citation rule
Why B? This is Retrieve failing, not Generate. The model did exactly what it was asked — answer from the given context, cite the source. The problem happened one step earlier: the search step handed it the wrong, incomplete context to begin with. Option D is a trap — the assistant's citation rule worked perfectly; it correctly cited the one report it was given. The rule didn't fail. The search that fed it did.
§ 2

Tool Demo — Gemini Notebook

⏱ 20 min

Here's the direct fix for that bug: a tool purpose-built for exactly the scale problem a growing pile of documents creates.

What Is Gemini Notebook?

Google's grounding tool. You upload sources — PDFs, Google Docs, websites, YouTube URLs, even raw pasted text — and it builds a private AI assistant that answers only from those sources, with citations back to the exact passage. Unlike a Claude Project or a raw chat, it has no access to the open web or its general training data when answering. If the answer isn't in your sources, it says so. It's completely free with a Google account.

🎙 Instructor Says

"The thing to remember about Gemini Notebook: it's intentionally limited. It will only answer from what you give it — and unlike dumping forty files into a Project, it's actually designed to search that pile properly. Watch me build one live. Then you'll build your own."

Use Gemini Notebook when...Just use a Claude Project or Gem when...
You have a large, specific pile of documents you want answers fromA Project's small, curated knowledge base is enough
You need citations you can verify before it reaches the coachYou're brainstorming or drafting
The topic is domain-specific (a player's history, a workload archive)The topic is broad guidance already in the Project instructions
Answers must not be made up, everApproximate is okay

Step-by-step demo (share screen)

Step 1 — Open Gemini Notebook. Go to notebooklm.google.com and sign in with any Google account. Click "+ Create new."

Step 2 — Name it. Call it Scouting Intelligence Notebook. It immediately prompts for sources — no question box until you add at least one.

Step 3 — Add sources. A PDF (the WACA Cricket Scoring Guide), a website (Sportsmith's fast-bowler workload article), and a YouTube video (Brett Lee's Fast Bowling Masterclass). Wait ~30 seconds per source.

Step 4 — Ask the first question. "Based on my sources, what's one framework recommended for limiting a young fast bowler's weekly overs?"

🎙 Instructor Says

"While these process — this is the moment. Gemini Notebook is reading each source, breaking it into chunks, and indexing them so it can retrieve the right one later, not just the first one it finds. This is the 'Retrieve' step of RAG happening automatically — the exact step that failed in our opening bug."

Step 5 — Check the citations

Every answer comes with numbered citations like [1]. Click one — Gemini Notebook jumps to the exact sentence in the exact source. The user in our opening bug would have caught it in one click if their tool had surfaced citations this precisely.

Step 6 — Test the refusal behaviour

Ask something not in your sources — "Who won the Ranji Trophy final in 2019?" — and watch it decline instead of guessing. A grounded tool that refuses is worth more than a clever one that lies.

Step 7 — The power features

📘 Study guide · 📋 Briefing doc (a ready-made briefing pack, auto-generated) · ❓ FAQ · 🎧 Audio Overview — two AI hosts discuss your sources, podcast-style.

Step 8 — Share it with the whole team

Click Share — Gemini Notebook shares exactly like a Google Doc: invite teammates and the coach by email, or generate a link. Viewers can ask questions themselves; they just can't add or remove sources.

🎙 Instructor Says

"This is the answer to 'how does the whole team use this, not just the person who set it up' — and it's built into the free tool you already have open, no new platform, nothing that can break mid-demo. One notebook, correctly grounded, one Share button, and teammates are asking questions against the exact same sources, without the owner being in the loop."

Beginner Note: Don't overthink the sources — a scoring guide PDF and two URLs is plenty to start.
Intermediate Note: Find a second workload article that recommends slightly different numbers, upload it alongside the first, then ask a disagreement question. Watch how Gemini Notebook handles the conflict.
Advanced Note: Gemini Notebook caps at 50 sources per notebook. Upload 15–20, then ask a synthesis question spanning 5+ sources. Notice retrieval quality start to degrade — that's the same trade-off that breaks a big Project, now visible on purpose.

Gemini Notebook Danger — More Sources Isn't More Context

Uploading 50 unrelated sources doesn't make the notebook smarter — it dilutes retrieval quality. 5–10 focused sources beat 50 random ones. If a Notebook stops citing correctly, prune the source pile first, before blaming the tool.

Quiz 2 · You upload the verified shortlist and three workload reports to Gemini Notebook, then ask: "What did the coach say about Rathi in last week's team meeting?" — a meeting that was never uploaded. What's the most likely and most desirable response?

AIt searches the internet for the coach's public comments
BIt generates a plausible answer based on the workload reports and the coach's likely views

Why C? Gemini Notebook doesn't access the internet and doesn't speculate. When the answer isn't there, it says so — exactly the discipline that would have caught the opening bug immediately, instead of weeks later in production. Option B is the hallucination trap: a confident answer sounding right while being completely invented.

Tool Demo — Perplexity + NoteGPT

⏱ 18 min

Gemini Notebook is RAG over your own filing cabinet. But sometimes you need what's happening in the cricket world right now — Gemini Notebook falls short there. Perplexity steps in.

PERPLEXITY · RAG FOR THE LIVE WEB

🌐 SIM 5 · ChatGPT vs Perplexity, Live

Same question, side by side — "What happened in this week's international cricket news?"

Demo walkthrough

Step 1 — Open perplexity.ai. No login required for the free tier.

Step 2 — Ask something time-sensitive: "What are the biggest storylines from this month's domestic cricket season?" Watch the inline [1] [2] [3] citations — hover to preview, click to open.

Step 4 — Toggle on Deep Research and ask a bigger question: "Compare how different national teams are handling bowler workload management in 2026." Perplexity runs ~20–30 searches and produces a cited report in 2–3 minutes.

Step 5 — Click Spaces → Create a Space. Add custom instructions (e.g. "always cite sources, always flag disagreements") and every thread inside follows those rules automatically — the same "set the rules once" idea as a Gemini Gem or Claude Project, except grounded in the live web and shareable with up to 10 collaborators.

Perplexity Danger — Citation ≠ Credibility

Perplexity cites — but not every cited source is credible. It sometimes retrieves opinion pieces, low-quality SEO farms, or Reddit threads presented alongside peer-reviewed research. Citation ≠ credibility. Always check the source domain before trusting.

Part B: NoteGPT — RAG for a Single Long Video

Imagine a full-length documentary on a legendary player's career that you want mined for scouting insight. Nobody has two hours to sit through it. NoteGPT does: paste a YouTube URL → transcribes the video → generates a timestamped summary and chapter notes → a chat box answers questions from the transcript, with a timestamp citation. It can also generate flashcards, a mind map, or a short quiz from the transcript.

🎬
Analogy · The Chunked Transcript

Ask NoteGPT "At what timestamp does the documentary discuss [a career turning point]?" and it answers with a clickable timestamp. That's the RAG pattern again — transcript chunked by time, relevant chunk retrieved, timestamp returned. A feature-length documentary becomes a 2-minute query.

Advanced Note: Chain NoteGPT → Gemini Notebook — copy the generated notes and paste them as a text source into a Gemini Notebook notebook, so the documentary becomes searchable alongside the scoring guide and the workload article. Same "preprocess then ground" pattern real production RAG pipelines use.

NoteGPT Danger — Garbage Transcript In, Garbage Summary Out

NoteGPT works from the auto-generated transcript, which can mis-hear words — especially names and technical terms. A summary based on a bad transcript inherits the errors. Spot-check timestamps for critical claims.

Quiz 3 · A team has 10 internal workload/scouting documents, 2 PDF match reports, and a 60-minute recording of the coach's tactical review on YouTube. An analyst wants to ask questions across all of it, with citations. Which combination is most appropriate?

APaste everything into one giant chat message
BUse Perplexity — it can search the internet for similar tactical patterns
CUse NoteGPT to generate notes from the video, then load everything into Gemini Notebook
DUse Gemini Notebook alone — it can't process YouTube, so skip the video
Why C? This is the tool-chaining pattern we just covered. NoteGPT structures the video content; Gemini Notebook then holds everything — video notes, workload docs, match reports — in one queryable, cited knowledge base. Option D undersells Gemini Notebook, which does support YouTube directly, but chaining through NoteGPT for long recordings often produces better structured notes. Option A hits context-window limits and gives zero citations. Option B is the wrong retrieval source entirely.

Exercise 4.2 — The False Premise Test

This is what AI teams call the false premise test. We deliberately ask something WRONG and see which tools catch it.

⚖️ SIM 7 · Grounded vs Ungrounded — Three Tools

Same false-premise question to all three: "Why is a bowler allowed to bowl two overs in a row from the same end in ODI cricket?" (This is false — overs must alternate between bowlers.)

🎙 Instructor Says

"A tool that plays along is dangerous in exactly the way the stale-report bug was dangerous — confidently wrong, and it takes real verification work to catch. A grounded tool hits a real source and course-corrects. That's why citations aren't optional — they're the safety net."

Quiz 4 · During Exercise 4.2, Perplexity corrected the false premise about bowling consecutive overs, but ChatGPT (web search off) confidently explained a rule that doesn't exist. Which best explains why a grounded tool is less likely to agree with a false premise?

AGrounded tools have larger models trained on more cricket data
BThe retrieval step surfaces real sources that contradict the false premise, anchoring the model to accurate information before it generates a response
CPerplexity is specifically programmed to detect cricket questions
DGrounded tools avoid answering rules-related questions
Why B? Perplexity retrieves real sources on cricket's laws before generating anything. Those sources say overs must alternate between bowlers. That retrieved content becomes the context the model generates from — contradicting the false premise before generation even starts. This is the same retrieval-first principle behind every fix we've covered today.

GEMINI NOTEBOOK

Challenge A · "The Report Room"

›

Scenario: You're done getting burned by stale reports. You want a personal grounded notebook that holds every workload report, the verified shortlist, and a nets-session video — with citations you can check before anything goes to the coach.

Mission

Build a Gemini Notebook notebook called "The Report Room."

Configure

1. Sources (at least 4, mixed types): 1 PDF (a scoring/rules guide, or your own mock workload report), 1 YouTube video (a bowling technique or nets-session video), 1 web URL (a workload or fitness article), 1 pasted-text source (mock scouting notes or a shortlist).
2. Ask three grounded questions: "Based on my sources, which players show a rising workload trend?" · "Across all my sources, what's the most commonly flagged risk area?" · "What would I miss if I only checked the most recent report? Cite examples."
3. Generate: a Briefing Doc + Audio Overview.
4. Share it. Click Share and either invite one classmate by email or generate a link.

Success Criteria

Every answer has clickable citations pointing to real passages in your sources. A generic, uncited answer means a source didn't upload correctly. You have a working Share link or invite sent.

PERPLEXITY

Challenge B · "The Opposition Desk"

›

Scenario: You need a fresh, sourced brief on the touring team before every selection meeting — team news, injury updates, recent form — and you're tired of an hour of manual reading every week.

Mission

Build a Perplexity Space called "Opposition Desk."

Configure

1. Open perplexity.ai → Spaces → Create a Space. Name it "Opposition Desk."
2. Custom instructions: always cite sources for every claim, always flag where sources disagree, always end with one sentence on what matters most for the selection meeting.
3. Turn on Deep Research and run: "Produce a briefing on the touring team's current form, covering the last 7 days only: team news, injury updates, and recent match results. Cite sources for every claim. Flag anything where sources disagree. End with one sentence on what matters most for the upcoming selection meeting."
4. Verify at least 2 citations by clicking them.
5. Note that Spaces support up to 10 collaborators — anyone invited can ask follow-ups in the same Space.

Success Criteria

Every claim in the brief has a citation, at least 2 verified as accurate, and the Space is set up to be reused next week with one click instead of retyping the prompt.

Key Takeaways

  1. RAG = retrieve → augment → generate. Give the AI the right notes before it answers — and make sure they're the right notes. Everything else is implementation detail.
  2. Memory and retrieval are different problems. A Gem or Project remembers its job forever. Whether it finds the right passage in a pile of forty files is a separate question — the one behind stale-report answers.
  3. "I don't know" is a feature. A grounded tool that refuses when it lacks sources is more trustworthy than a clever one that lies confidently.
  4. Different grounding tools for different source shapes. Your files, quick answers → Gemini Notebook. The live web, recurring questions → Perplexity, saved as a Space. A video → NoteGPT.
  5. A Claude Project isn't wrong, it's just opaque. It doesn't show you which files it searched or why it picked one report over another. Gemini Notebook's citations are the closest thing to X-ray vision you get without leaving free, no-code tools.
  6. These tools handle the vast majority of grounding needs. You only need a fully custom RAG pipeline once you outgrow what a free, citation-first, no-code tool can do — and today gave you a very high ceiling before you hit that point.

The Retrieval Fix — In Action

Picture the fix at work. A team rebuilds its Scouting Intelligence Notebook properly — every workload report uploaded, nothing missing — and shares it with everyone. A teammate opens the shared link and asks the exact same question that broke the assistant earlier: "What's Arjun Rathi's workload trend over his last 6 reports?" Five reports come back. Each one cited. Each one checkable in one click. Nobody needed the notebook's owner to rebuild anything — the shared notebook was already sitting there, already correct. Instead of sending a frustrated message, the analyst forwards the citation straight to the coach.

"Give the AI the right notes — and make sure they're the right ones."
