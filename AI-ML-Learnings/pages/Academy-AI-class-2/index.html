Section 1 · 5 minWhere we are: it talks, now make it say something useful

Last class you made a model reply to you in code. That is the plumbing, and the plumbing is the easy half.

Here is the part nobody tells you. The gap between a useless AI answer and one you would ship almost never comes down to which model you picked. Two people on the same model, the same free tier, the same task, get completely different results.

Section 2 · 11 minThe prompt is the product

Before any technique, the reason this matters.

A lot of the AI products people pay for are, underneath, the same model you are calling free from your laptop. No special training, no secret model. What they sell is the prompt wrapped around your input, and a place to type.

That is not a criticism. It is the opportunity. If the prompt is what makes the product good, prompt writing is product work, and you can do it today with no budget.

Step 1 and Step 2: build a product out of one line

Note for InstructorLive demo, 6 minutes. Two chat windows, or one window and a new chat. Same model both times. This does not depend on the model failing, so it reproduces every batch.
1
In the first window, paste the page text and type: Summarise this. Read the output to the room. It will be competent, shapeless, and include the navigation menu or the advertisement at least some of the time.
2
In the second window, paste this first, then the page text. Read the output.
You summarise web pages for people who are deciding whether to read them.

Every summary you write:
- opens with the single most useful fact, not a description of the page
- is under 80 words
- uses plain language a commuter would use
- keeps any number that affects a decision: times, fares, dates, distances
- ignores navigation menus, adverts, cookie notices and "also read" links

If the page does not say something, do not fill the gap. Say the page
does not mention it.
Ask LearnersWhich of those two would you put in front of a user of your app?
ImportantNothing changed except the text above the page. Same model, same free key, same input. That block is the entire product, and by the end of today it is sitting in your summarizer.py.
Doubt by LearnerIf it is just a prompt, what stops a competitor copying it?

Very little, and that is true of most software. What is hard to copy is knowing which rules to write, which comes from watching real users be disappointed. The prompt is cheap to copy and expensive to get right.

Section 3 · 16 minRCTFC: build your summariser's prompt

Five parts. Write all five and most prompts come out right the first time.

Part What it answers In your summariser
R Role Who is writing You summarise web pages for people deciding whether to read them
C Context What is going on, who reads it Scraped page text, shown in an app to someone in a hurry
T Task The one thing to do Summarise this page
F Format The exact shape of the answer One opening line, then up to four bullets
C Constraints The limits and the rules Under 80 words, keep the numbers, ignore the nav, do not invent

Writing a prompt is writing a good bug report. "The app is broken" gets you nothing. Steps to reproduce, what you expected, what happened, which browser, gets you a fix. Same reader, completely different outcome.

Build it live, one part at a time

Note for InstructorLive demo, 8 minutes, and this is the core of the class. Start with the task alone and add one part per run. Read a line or two of each output rather than the whole thing. Do not paste the finished five-part prompt and explain it afterwards. Building it is the lesson.
1
Task only: Summarise this page. Output is readable and shapeless, and usually swallows the navigation menu.
2
Add Context: say it is scraped text from a news page, shown to someone deciding whether to read the article. The output starts addressing a reader instead of nobody.
3
Add Format: ask for one opening line and up to four bullets. The output becomes scannable.
4
Add Constraints: under 80 words, keep every number that affects a decision, ignore navigation and adverts, and never add a fact the page does not contain. Now it stops padding and stops inventing.
5
Add Role: you summarise pages for people in a hurry. The tone finally sounds like a product rather than a book report.
Ask LearnersWhich of those five changed the output most? There is no right answer and the disagreement is the interesting part.
Note for InstructorAdvanced learner extension, use only if the room is ahead: Format and Constraints do most of the work on routine tasks, and Role matters most when tone is the deliverable. Say it and move on. Do not turn it into a ranking exercise.
ImportantOrder does not matter much. Completeness does. A prompt missing Format is the single most common reason people say the AI did not do what they asked.
Doubt by LearnerDoes this mean every prompt has to be long?

No. It means every prompt has to be complete. "Summarise this page in 3 bullets for someone deciding whether to read it" is short and has Task, Format, Constraints and Context in it. Short and complete beats long and vague.

Section 4 · 7 minRole prompting

Role is the part people skip, and it is the cheapest way to change tone and depth in one line.

A role does two things. It sets the vocabulary, and it sets what the model thinks is worth mentioning. Summarise the metro page as a commuter and you get times and fares. As a civil engineer and you get elevated stations and the interchange. As an investor and you get passenger numbers. Same page.

Note for InstructorLive demo, 3 minutes. Run the same summarise task with three different roles back to back, reading only the first two sentences of each. The shift is immediate, which is why this demo always lands. Then ask which role suits the app you are building, and let them argue briefly.
ImportantA role changes emphasis and tone. It does not add knowledge the model does not have, and it does not make the model qualified. "You are a doctor" changes how an answer sounds, not whether it is safe to act on. Say this plainly; learners do over-trust role prompts.
Ask LearnersWhat role would you give your summariser? Type it as one line starting with "You are" or "You summarise".
Doubt by Learner (Optional)Is "You are an expert" worth adding?

Barely. It is too vague to change what the model emphasises. A role earns its place when it implies an audience and a vocabulary. "You summarise pages for people deciding whether to read them" does work. "You are an expert" does not.

Section 5 · 11 minZero-shot and few-shot

Zero-shot is what you have been doing all class: instructions, no examples. It is enough for most things.

Few-shot is when you paste two or three examples of the output you want, then ask for one more. You are not describing the format any more. You are showing it.

The analogy: telling a new joiner "follow our code style" versus opening three of your best pull requests and saying "write it like this". Same person, completely different first attempt.

When examples beat instructions

Use few-shot when the format is specific to you and hard to describe. You could spend a paragraph explaining the house style of your summaries, or paste two real ones.

Note for InstructorLive demo, 5 minutes. Run the summary with instructions only, then again with the two examples below. The second run copies the shape exactly, including the dash, the sentence rhythm and the way numbers are written, none of which you described. That mimicry is the point and it reproduces reliably.
Summarise the page below.
Match the shape of these examples exactly.

---
Cheaper and faster from Monday, if you are going to Hinjawadi.
- Opens Monday, 06:00 to 22:00 on weekdays
- Rs 10 to Rs 45, trains every 8 minutes at peak
- 21 of 23 stations open now, two more in March
---
Worth it only if you book before the 30th.
- Early fare ends 30 Nov, Rs 1,200 after
- Three sessions, two hours each, online
- Recording available for 60 days
---
ImportantTwo or three examples is the sweet spot. One example and the model treats it as a one-off. Ten and you are paying for tokens you did not need. If quality is still off at three, your examples probably disagree with each other.
Doubt by LearnerDoes few-shot teach the model permanently?

No. The examples live in that one prompt and are gone the moment the call ends. Nothing is saved, nothing is trained. This is the no-memory point from Class 1: if you want the examples every time, they have to be in the system prompt your code sends every time, which is exactly what you will do in Section 10.

Section 6 · 9 minStructured outputs: asking for JSON

Everything so far produced text for a person. Next class your summary goes into a web interface, and the class after that people start wiring these into automations. The moment a program reads the output, the shape has to be exact.

Ask for JSON and state the shape.

Return the summary as JSON with exactly these keys:

{
  "headline": "Pune Metro Line 3 opens Monday",
  "bullets": ["...", "..."],
  "numbers_mentioned": ["Rs 10 to Rs 45", "06:00 to 22:00"],
  "page_type": "news"
}

Rules:
- bullets: between 2 and 4 items, each under 15 words
- numbers_mentioned: copy figures exactly as written on the page, or []
- page_type: one of news, product, blog, documentation, other
- Return only the JSON object. No explanation, no code fences, no preamble.
ImportantThat last line is the one people forget, and it is why their app breaks. Without it you get a friendly sentence before the JSON, which is not valid JSON, and json.loads fails on character one.
Note for InstructorLive demo, 4 minutes. Run it once without the "return only" line and show the chatty preamble. Then add the line and run again. This is a failure you create on purpose rather than hope for, so it works in every batch.
Doubt by LearnerWhat if it returns JSON that is almost right?

Tighten the spec rather than fixing the output by hand. Name every key, say what type each one is, and say what to do when the information is missing. Most malformed output comes from a case you did not tell it how to handle, and "use an empty list if there are none" removes the most common one.

Note for InstructorAdvanced learner extension, use only if the room is ahead: the paid APIs can enforce a schema so the response is guaranteed to parse rather than merely asked to. Name it, say it is the same idea with a guarantee, and move on.

Section 7 · 8 minChain-of-thought, and when it still matters

Chain-of-thought is one instruction: ask the model to work through the problem step by step before answering.

ImportantThis technique has aged, and the honest version is this. A model that is already thinking before it answers does this on its own, and telling it to think step by step adds nothing. A model that is not thinking has no such step, and there the instruction still helps a lot.

On gpt-oss that is a dial rather than two different models. Your summariser runs at low reasoning effort, because summarising has no dependent steps, and low effort is exactly where this technique still earns its place.

So chain-of-thought is now a decision, not a habit. Use it when the model is not already thinking for you, and the task has steps that depend on each other.

Where it earns its place

Summarising a clean article does not need it. Summarising a messy page does.

Ask the model at low effort to summarise the metro page and it usually does fine. Ask it to first separate the article from the navigation and adverts, then identify which numbers affect a reader's decision, then write the summary from only those, and the result is noticeably tighter on a cluttered page.

Note for InstructorLive demo, 4 minutes, at low reasoning effort. Run the plain summary, then the staged version with the three steps named. Compare what each one kept. Do not claim the first is wrong; it is usually fine and shallow, and shallow is the thing to point at.
Ask LearnersFor your own work, name one task where you would want to see the model's steps rather than just its answer.

Section 8 · 8 minSelf-critique: let it mark its own work

A model is better at spotting a problem in finished text than at avoiding it while writing. That asymmetry is free quality.

The pattern is three moves: draft, critique, revise.

Write the summary.

Then, before showing me anything, check your own draft against these:
- Is every number that affects a decision still in it?
- Did you include anything that is not on the page?
- Did any navigation, advert or "also read" text leak in?
- Is it under 80 words?

List what failed, fix it, and show me only the corrected summary.
ImportantThe checklist is what makes this work. "Check your work and improve it" produces a paragraph of self-congratulation. Named checks produce real catches, because each one is a question with a yes or no answer.
Note for InstructorLive demo, 4 minutes, and build it rather than handing it over. Run the plain summary. Ask the room what is wrong with it. Turn their answers into the checklist live, in the prompt. Run again. A checklist the room wrote catching the problems the room found is far more convincing than one that arrived finished.
Ask LearnersGive me one check that belongs on almost every prompt you write. Expect "did you make anything up" and take it.
Doubt by LearnerIs the model actually catching mistakes, or just saying it did?

Both happen, which is why you ask for the list of what failed. If the critique names a specific line and the revision changes that line, it did the work. If it says "improved clarity and flow" and nothing moved, it did not, and you need sharper checks.

Section 9 · 7 minPrompt chaining: one job per prompt

When a prompt tries to do four things, it usually does the first well and the rest badly.

Chaining splits the work. Each prompt does one job and hands its output to the next.

For your app: first prompt strips the page down to the article and pulls out the facts as JSON. Second prompt turns that JSON into the summary a person reads. The facts cannot drift between them, because the second prompt never sees the messy page.

Note for InstructorLive demo, 3 minutes. Run the extraction, then feed its output into the summary prompt. Point out that the summary is now working from clean structured facts rather than a page full of adverts. That is the architecture of next class's app, so name it as such.
ImportantThe test for whether to chain: if you can describe the task with the word "and" between two verbs, it is probably two prompts. Extract and summarise. Clean and format. Find and fix.
Note for InstructorAdvanced learner extension: chaining by hand is tedious, which is the entire reason automation tools exist. In Class 6 you build this kind of chain in n8n, where the output of one step drops into the next as a variable. That is also where template prompting with variables belongs, so if a learner asks about reusable prompt templates today, point them there.

Section 10 · 12 minPut it in code: summarizer.py

Note for InstructorHands-on, 12 minutes. This is the handover from chat to code, and it is the whole reason the class was in a chat window until now. Everyone should leave with this file saved and working.

You have spent an hour getting a prompt right in a chat window, where the loop is three seconds. Now it goes where it will live.

What this code does

Three things. It holds your prompt as a string. It sends that string as the system message and the page text as the user message. It returns the reply.

That is the same four-step shape from Class 1, with your prompt dropped into the system slot. Nothing new is happening.

# summarizer.py
# Your prompt, now in the place that runs it.

import os
from dotenv import load_dotenv
from openai import OpenAI

load_dotenv()

client = OpenAI(
    api_key=os.getenv("GROQ_API_KEY"),
    base_url="https://api.groq.com/openai/v1",
)

# Everything you wrote in Sections 2 to 8 goes here, between the triple quotes.
SYSTEM_PROMPT = """You summarise web pages for people who are deciding
whether to read them.

Every summary you write:
- opens with the single most useful fact, not a description of the page
- is under 80 words
- uses plain language a commuter would use
- keeps any number that affects a decision: times, fares, dates, distances
- ignores navigation menus, adverts, cookie notices and "also read" links

If the page does not say something, do not fill the gap. Say the page
does not mention it."""


def summarize(page_text):
    response = client.chat.completions.create(
        model="openai/gpt-oss-120b",
        temperature=0.2,            # low: a summary should not vary run to run
        reasoning_effort="low",     # summarising has no dependent steps
        messages=[
            {"role": "system", "content": SYSTEM_PROMPT},
            {"role": "user",   "content": f"Summarise this page:\n\n{page_text}"},
        ],
    )
    return response.choices[0].message.content


# Next class this text arrives from a real web page. For now, paste some in.
if __name__ == "__main__":
    sample = """<paste the metro page text here>"""
    print(summarize(sample))

Ask LearnersWho has a summary printing in their terminal? Yes in the chat.
ImportantNotice the temperature is 0.2, not the default. Class 1 taught the dial; this is the first time you have used it for a reason. A summary of the same page should not change between runs, so you want the model near the top of its probability list every time.
Note for InstructorThe two common failures are the triple-quoted string broken by a stray quote in the learner's own prompt, and the file saved outside the folder with the .env. Check those first.
Note for InstructorIf anyone is on an old openai package and gets an unexpected keyword error on reasoning_effort, deleting that one line is harmless: the model simply uses its default effort. Tell them to run pip install -U openai after class.
Doubt by LearnerWhy is the page text in the user message and the prompt in the system message? Could I not put both together?

You could, and it would mostly work. Keeping them separate matters for a reason you will meet properly next class: the page text is untrusted. It comes from the internet, and anyone can put anything on a web page, including instructions aimed at your model. Keeping your rules in the system slot and the page in the user slot is the first line of that defence.

Section 11 · 4 minWrap-up and what is next

The techniques, and when to reach for each

Technique Reach for it when
RCTFC Always. It is the baseline, not a technique
Role Tone or audience keeps coming out wrong
Few-shot The format is yours and hard to describe in words
Structured output A program reads the result, not a person
Chain-of-thought The model is not thinking first, or you want to steer which steps happen
Self-critique A mistake costs more than a second pass
Chaining The task description has "and" between two verbs

The checklist to keep

Before you send a prompt, check you have said who is writing, what is going on, the one thing to do, the shape of the answer, and the limits. Then the two people forget: what should happen when information is missing, and whether anything other than the answer is allowed in the output.
