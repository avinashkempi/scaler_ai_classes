Class 5 solved a real problem — Scout Assist wasn't hallucinating anymore, it was citing real reports, and the right five, not the wrong one. That was a genuine win. But "it cited something" and "the citation is trustworthy" are two different claims, and Karan just walked straight into the gap between them.

Meet The Trust Problem

A citation is a pointer, not a guarantee. It tells you where an answer came from — not whether that source is current, whether it's credible, or whether other sources even agree with it. Perplexity cited a real article — from five months ago, published well before Curran's injury was even public. ChatGPT with no web access cited nothing at all, and guessed instead. Gemini Notebook did the responsible thing and refused, because nobody had uploaded anything about this player. All three behaved exactly as designed. None of them told Karan what he actually needed: which answer to trust, tonight.

This same gap — a citation that looks verified but isn't worth trusting — scales far beyond one scouting question. Every grounded tool inherits the credibility of whatever it happens to retrieve, and the failure looks identical whether it's Karan's stale citation or something the whole internet sees at once.

Why this matters for you — 2026 reality check

  • Every research or analyst job increasingly means judging AI-sourced answers, not just producing them.
  • Perplexity, Claude, and AI Studio all format answers to look verified — formatting is not the same as verification.
  • Google AI Overviews (2024) — told users glue helps cheese stick to pizza, citing a decade-old joke post as if it were a cooking tip.
  • Contract, legal, medical, and financial research is exactly where "cited but stale or wrong" causes real damage — same shape as Karan's fitness report, much higher stakes.
  • If you're using AI for real research, grading a citation is the actual skill now — the tools have already solved "can it cite."

By the end of today, you won't just know how to get a cited answer. You'll know how to tell whether it's one worth acting on.

§ 1

The Source-Quality Framework

⏱ 13 min

Here's the fix — and like Class 5's fix, it's simpler than it sounds. Four questions, asked of every citation before you act on it.

CITATION ≠ CREDIBILITY · THE FOUR CHECKS
CheckQuestionWhat Karan's Perplexity Citation Reveals
RecencyWhen was this published or last updated?Dated five months ago — before the injury update existed
AuthorityPrimary source, named journalist, or random aggregator?A syndicated aggregator site, not the club or a byline reporter
CorroborationDo other independent sources agree?ChatGPT's guess and Perplexity's citation actively disagree
RelevanceDoes it answer THIS question, or something adjacent?Talks about pre-season fitness, not current match fitness

Quiz 1 · Perplexity cited a real, on-topic sports article about Curran's fitness — but the article was published five months before his injury was even public. Which check did it fail?

AAuthority — the site isn't a credible publisher
BRecency — the source predates the information Karan actually needs
CRelevance — the article isn't about Curran at all
DCorroboration — no other source was checked
Why B? The article is on-topic (Relevance passes) and from a real sports outlet (Authority passes) — the problem is purely timing. It was accurate when published; it's outdated now. A citation can pass three checks and still be dangerous if it fails Recency, exactly what happened to Karan.
§ 2

Tool Demo — Claude Web Search

⏱ 18 min

First new tool today. You've been prompting Claude since Class 3 — Scout Assist's system prompt lives inside a Claude Project. What you haven't used yet: Claude can ground its own answers in a live web search, inside that exact same chat.

What Is Claude Web Search?

Claude can call a live web search mid-conversation when the tool is enabled, retrieve real pages, and cite them inline — the same RAG shape as Perplexity, but built into the general-purpose assistant you already use for everything else. No separate app, no context switch.

The thing to watch for: Claude doesn't always search. If it thinks it already knows the answer, it may respond straight from training data with no search at all — and nothing on screen shouts a warning about that. Try triggering it yourself in the simulator below — once with search on, once with it off.

Use Claude Web Search when...Use Perplexity when...
You're already mid-conversation and need one grounded fact, no context switchYou need Focus modes, Deep Research, or a saved recurring Space
The question is a side-quest inside a longer, non-research taskResearch IS the task, start to finish
You want the same assistant holding your Project's rules to also check itself against the webYou want a dedicated research tool built only for this

Step-by-step demo

Step 1 — Open claude.ai. Start a new chat, or reuse a Project.

Step 2 — Check the Search toggle in the tools menu — enable web search if it isn't already on.

Step 3 — Ask Karan's exact question again: "What's Sam Curran's fitness and injury status right now?"

Step 4 — Look for the inline citation markers and the small "searched the web" indicator. Click a citation to open the source.

Step 5 — Ask a follow-up needing a second search: "Now check if he's played any cricket since the injury." Watch Claude decide to search again.

🔎 SIM 3 · Claude, Search Off vs On

Same question. Toggle the search tool off and on.

Beginner Note: Enabling search is one click in the tools menu — no extra account or setup beyond Claude itself.
Intermediate Note: Ask a compound question spanning two live facts (fitness AND a rival bid) and watch Claude run multiple searches inside one turn.
Advanced Note: Claude sometimes cites a source without fully quoting it. Click through and read the actual page before trusting the paraphrase — the same discipline the Four Checks demand of every tool today.

Claude Web Search Danger — The Silent Non-Search

Claude can decide a question doesn't need a search and answer from training data instead — with no visible warning that it skipped the live web. No citation markers and no sources panel means no retrieval happened. Always check for that indicator before trusting a time-sensitive answer.

Quiz 2 · Karan asks Claude about Curran's fitness and gets a confident, well-written answer — but no citation markers and no sources panel anywhere. What should he conclude?

AThe answer is grounded, but Claude just hides its citations sometimes
BClaude likely answered from training data without searching — treat it as ungrounded and verify elsewhere
CClaude never needs to search for factual questions like this
DThe absence of citations means the information is definitely wrong
Why B? No citation markers or sources panel is the tell that no search ran — Claude silently chose to answer from its training data. That doesn't make the answer wrong, but it makes it unverifiable, which is functionally the same problem as any confident answer with nothing behind it to check.
§ 3

Tool Demo — AI Studio Google Grounding

⏱ 16 min

Second new tool — and this one pulls back the curtain Class 5 said stays hidden in Gemini Notebook, Perplexity, and Claude. Google AI Studio lets you flip on the exact retrieval switch and watch it work.

What Is AI Studio Grounding?

AI Studio (aistudio.google.com) is the developer playground for Gemini models. One toggle — "Grounding with Google Search" — makes any prompt search-backed, returning inline citations plus something the consumer tools never show: the actual search queries the model ran, and a support/confidence indicator per claim.

Step-by-step demo

Step 1 — Open aistudio.google.com, sign in with a Google account, open a new Chat prompt.

Step 2 — Find the Tools panel and enable "Grounding with Google Search."

Step 3 — Ask the same fitness question: "What's Sam Curran's fitness and injury status right now?"

Step 4 — Click into the grounding metadata — the literal search queries Gemini ran, the retrieved chunks, and the "Google Search Suggestions" widget (required to display if you ever ship this in a real product).

Step 5 — Turn grounding OFF, re-ask the identical prompt. Compare.

This is the one place today where you're not just trusting a citation — you can literally read the search query the model typed before it answered. That's the retrieval step from Class 5, made visible on purpose.

🛠 SIM 4 · Grounding Off vs On

Same prompt, same model. Toggle the Google Search grounding switch.

Beginner Note: This is the most "developer" tool of the five — expect a plainer interface than the consumer apps. That's normal.
Intermediate Note: Try a false-premise question here too, the same test Class 5 ran on Perplexity — grounding should correct it the same way.
Advanced Note: The grounding metadata — search queries, per-claim confidence — is exactly what a custom RAG pipeline would expose if you built one from scratch with an LLM API. This is the closest free tool gets to showing you the retrieval settings Class 5 said none of the no-code tools reveal.

AI Studio Danger — Grounding Is Off By Default, Per Prompt

Unlike Perplexity (almost always searches) or Gemini Notebook (always source-restricted), AI Studio's grounding toggle can be missed per session or per model. An ungrounded answer here looks nearly identical to a grounded one at a glance — the missing citations and metadata block are your only tell.

Quiz 3 · What can AI Studio's Grounding tool show you that Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini Notebook all keep hidden?

AIt never requires an API key or Google account
BIt's the only one of the five that works with YouTube videos
CThe actual search queries the model ran, plus a per-claim confidence score
DIt's the only free option among the five tools
Why C? Every other tool today hides its retrieval mechanics behind a clean citation. AI Studio is built for developers, so it exposes the literal search queries and a confidence indicator per claim — the retrieval internals Class 5 explicitly said the no-code tools keep invisible.

Quiz 4 · You ask the same question across three tools. Tool A gives a confident, well-written answer with no citation at all. Tool B cites an article published two days ago that you can click and read yourself. Tool C cites a source from several months ago. Which should you trust for a time-sensitive brief?

ATool A — a confident, well-written answer doesn't need a citation to be trusted
BTool B — it passes Recency with a source you can verify yourself; Tool A's uncited answer can't be checked at all, and Tool C's source is too old
CAll three equally — they all "answered," so they're equally trustworthy
DNone of them — a cited answer is never worth trusting
Why B? A citation you can check beats a confident answer you can't, and a recent citation beats a stale one. Tool B passes Recency with a source you can click and verify. Tool A's answer might be accurate, but with no citation it fails the very first requirement of any time-sensitive brief: something you can check before acting on it.

Mini Build — Ship The Due-Diligence Brief

⏱ 19 min

This is the main event. Two challenges — build the brief Karan actually owes the coach, running the framework for real. Own research task, need Deep Research or a recurring Space → Perplexity. A quick grounded fact inside a broader chat → Claude. Want to see the actual retrieval mechanics → AI Studio. Pick the challenge that matches what you'd actually use, or design your own.

PERPLEXITY / CLAUDE

Challenge A · "Karan's Due-Diligence Brief"

›

Scenario: Karan needs the actual brief for the coach by Thursday night — fitness, form, and contract situation on a target signing, every claim graded, not just cited.

Mission

Build a sourced due-diligence brief using Perplexity's Deep Research or a multi-turn Claude web-search conversation.

Configure

1. Run the research: "Produce a brief on [a real or mock player]'s current fitness, form, and contract status, covering the last 14 days only. Cite a source for every claim."
2. For every claim in the output, run the Four Checks — Recency, Authority, Corroboration, Relevance.
3. Flag or drop any claim that fails Recency or Authority.
4. End the brief with one explicit confidence line per claim: "high confidence" or "verify before acting."

Success Criteria

Every claim has a citation AND a visible pass/fail against the framework. At least one weak citation is caught and flagged rather than silently included.

AI STUDIO

Challenge B · "Vikram's Freshness Check"

›

Scenario: Vikram wants to double-check a rule or points-table fact using the one tool that shows its actual work, not just a citation.

Mission

Use AI Studio's Grounding toggle to verify a time-sensitive fact, then prove the grounding mattered.

Configure

1. Open aistudio.google.com, enable Grounding with Google Search.
2. Ask a time-sensitive question (a current standing, a recent rule clarification, a recent injury update).
3. Inspect the grounding metadata — the actual search queries run and the confidence indicator.
4. Turn grounding OFF and re-ask the identical prompt. Compare the two answers side by side.

Success Criteria

A written one-paragraph note on what changed with grounding on, quoting the actual search-query metadata as evidence — not just "it seemed more accurate."

Key Takeaways

  1. Citation ≠ credibility. Class 5 solved "did it make this up." Today solved "should you believe what it found."
  2. Four Checks, every time: Recency, Authority, Corroboration, Relevance. A citation can pass three and still fail one — and one failure is enough to hold off.
  3. Grounded tools differ in transparency, not just source. AI Studio shows its retrieval work; Claude can silently skip search; know which one you're holding before you trust it.
  4. "I don't know" is still a feature — but it doesn't answer urgent questions. Pick the tool that matches the deadline, not just the philosophy.
  5. Five tools, five shapes of research: own files → Gemini Notebook. Recurring, deep live research → Perplexity. One long video → NoteGPT. A quick grounded fact mid-conversation → Claude. Need to see the actual retrieval mechanics → AI Studio.
  6. This is the real bottleneck in "AI does my research now." Not generating an answer — judging one.
