When the Data Fights Back

Prepare for messy inputs, missing files and unreliable services. This pre-read introduces habits that keep a small data pipeline from silently losing records or inventing results.

About 35–40 minutesRead before classQuick checks included

1. Welcome

The goal shifts from making a project work once to making it behave predictably when reality is messy. Files can be missing, rows malformed, services slow, and retries can repeat work.

What to focus on
Understand the mental model first; you do not need to memorise every detail before class.

2. The big idea: fail visibly

A silent failure can be worse than a visible one. If bad records are skipped without being recorded, totals may look trustworthy while being wrong. Make clear what succeeded, failed and needs attention.

3. Files and paths

A relative path is interpreted from the working directory, which may differ from the script's folder. Know where inputs and outputs live, check whether files exist, and provide useful errors instead of assuming a file was created.

4. Validate before processing

Check required fields, types, ranges and formats where untrusted data enters. Decide whether a bad record is rejected, quarantined or repaired under a documented rule; do not silently guess.

5. Counting and reconciliation

Track input, successful, rejected and failed records. For example, 100 input rows might become 94 processed, 4 rejected and 2 failed. If the numbers do not reconcile, investigate before trusting the output.

6. Retries and idempotency

Retries help with temporary network failures, but repeating a payment or write can duplicate an action. An idempotent operation can be repeated without unintended extra effects. Use bounded retries, timeouts and logs.

7. Before class: inspect your project

Confirm you can run a Python file and know where input and output files are stored. Think of one failure your current project handles poorly. No new service or paid account should be necessary unless your instructor says otherwise.

  • □ Confirm the tools or accounts listed above.
  • □ Write down one question or failure case to discuss in class.
  • □ Keep credentials and secrets private.

8. Check yourself

Be ready to distinguish a missing file from a malformed record, explain reconciled counts, and describe why retries need special care around operations that change state.

8. Check yourself

Try these questions without looking back. Feedback appears after you choose an answer.

1. A pipeline reads 100 rows, processes 94, rejects 4 and reports 2 failures. What should happen?

2. Why can retrying a payment be dangerous?
