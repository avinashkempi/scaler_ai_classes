The Analyst Files · Case #08
THE BUDGET SPLIT
E-Commerce

Setup

The marketing lead drops by: “We’ve got ₹50,000 for this quarter’s campaign. Over 2,000 customers. That budget doesn’t stretch to everyone — so who do we actually spend it on?”

Evidence

  • The company has 27,000+ order-level transactions in a raw database — plenty of data, but no segmentation yet.
  • Blasting the same offer to every customer wastes budget on people who’ll never buy and under-serves those about to churn.
  • What’s needed is a way to group customers by value and risk using nothing but their purchase history.

The Mystery

What three signals from the transaction log alone would you use to judge how valuable each customer is?

Make Your Move

Choose your first move before continuing. There’s no need to know the answer yet — tap an option to see why it’s worth considering.

Why this move

RFM stands for Recency (lower is better), Frequency (higher is better), and Monetary value (higher is better). You’ll compute all three in Google Sheets, score each 1–3 using percentiles, concatenate into an RFM code, and map it to nine named customer profiles — from Champions to About-to-Sleep.

Your mission: come to class knowing what a percentile is.

🎯 Your mission: come to class with a first move in mind.

Section 2

Real-World Use Cases

Marketing budget allocationInstead of splitting ₹50K evenly across 2,000 customers, spend the bulk on Champions and “Can’t Lose Them” — same budget, dramatically more return.
Churn preventionRFM’s Recency score flags customers going quiet before they fully churn — the “Can’t Lose Them” segment is the early-warning system.
Personalized campaignsChampions get exclusive early access; Hibernating customers get win-back discounts — different actions for different segments, all from the same data.
Interview frameworksRFM is one of the most-asked frameworks in product/data analyst interviews — knowing how to build it from raw transactions is a table-stakes skill.

Prerequisites to Refresh

Make sure these concepts are fresh — CRM Analytics builds directly on your spreadsheet and analytical foundations.

Percentiles
You’ll use PERCENTILE.EXC to split customers into scoring bands. Refresh what the 33rd and 66th percentiles mean.
Google Sheets basics
COUNTIF, SUMIF, IF/nested-IF, CONCATENATE, Pivot Tables — you’ll use all of these to build the RFM pipeline.
TROPiCS segmentation instinct
From L1 — slicing a metric by user attributes to diagnose a change. Same move, different goal: diagnosis then, targeting now.
Structured decomposition
The habit of breaking a big question into smaller pieces (from guesstimates) applies here: big customer base → segments → targeted actions.
