The Growth Curve — One Zap, Three Stages

Same Zap the whole way through. Nothing gets thrown away and rebuilt; each stage adds to the last. Notice the shape immediately: Zapier is a numbered vertical list, not a canvas. The Vocabulary, In Thirty Seconds

Zapier renames almost everything you learned last class. Same concepts, different words — and you will hear these words in job descriptions.

Zapier wordWhat it meansLast class you called it
ZapOne automation, start to finishA workflow
TriggerStep 1. Always exactly one, always firstA trigger node
ActionEvery step after the firstAny other node
TaskOne successful action step. This is the unit you are billed inNothing — n8n had no equivalent
FilterA step that stops the Zap when a condition isn't metClosest thing: the IF node — but this one doesn't branch
PathsThe actual fork — separate branches with their own stepsThe IF node's two outputs
Zap HistoryEvery run, its status, and the data at each stepThe Executions tab
PublishTurning the Zap on for realThe Active toggle — and it bites exactly the same way
🧾
The One Analogy

n8n is a workshop: a bench, parts everywhere, and you can build anything if you know what you're doing. Zapier is a recipe card: numbered steps, one per line, in order — deliberately less powerful, and readable by anyone who wanders into the kitchen. You do not hand a colleague a workshop. You hand them a recipe.

Insider fact — say this, it reframes the whole class: plenty of professional automation people run both, on purpose. n8n for the heavy, custom, data-shaped work they maintain themselves; Zapier for anything a colleague needs to read, own, or change without them. Choosing tools per job — and being able to defend the choice — reads as far more senior than being loyal to one.
Instructor — before you teach this live [read this the week before, not the morning of]: Zapier's free plan is limited to two-step Zaps, and Filters, multi-step and Paths sit on paid tiers — but every new account gets a free trial of the paid features for a couple of weeks. That trial is what makes today's class possible. Tell learners in the pre-class message: create the Zapier account a day early, and do not burn the trial before class. Verify the current plan limits on zapier.com/pricing the week you teach — this changes, and getting it wrong strands the room at Zap 2. Also pre-connect Google Sheets, Google Chat, Groq and Notion on your own account before you share screen; four OAuth/API-key connections live is eleven minutes of nothing.
No Google Chat access? Fine — and say this out loud at minute 7. Any Google account can create a Space at chat.google.com in under a minute, no admin approval needed — but a school or work Google Workspace account occasionally has Spaces switched off by an org policy. If anyone hits that wall, they use Gmail → Send Email to themselves everywhere this script says Google Chat, and every single lesson still lands. The blueprint says "tell the team" — that's a role, not a brand. Never let a learner sit blocked on an app choice that was never the point.

Look at what just happened to the architecture. Kabir's n8n workflow writes a row. Farah's Zap reads that row. Neither one knows the other exists — the spreadsheet is the handshake. Live build — three moves (share screen, build alongside)

Move 1 · Pick the trigger.

1. In Zapier, click Create → Zaps → New Zap. A blank Zap opens with one empty step already sitting there, labelled Trigger.
2. Click that empty step. Type Google Sheets into the search box and click it.
3. Under Trigger Event, choose New or Updated Spreadsheet Row, then click Continue.
4. Connect your Google account — sign in, or pick the account already connected — then Continue.
5. Two dropdowns appear: pick the Spreadsheet, then the Worksheet (the leads tab), then Continue.
6. Click Test trigger. This is the moment to slow down — do not click past it.

Say this while the test spins: Zapier just went and fetched a real row from the real sheet. That row is now sample data for the rest of the build, which is why every field you need appears in a dropdown from here on. n8n made you learn expression syntax to reach data. Zapier hands you the data and asks you to point at it. Same problem, opposite ergonomics — and that trade is the whole tool in one sentence.
Before Move 2 — a one-time setup, done inside Google Chat itself, not Zapier. Google Chat won't let any outside tool post into a space until you've explicitly installed it there.
a. Open Google Chat (chat.google.com) and open the space you want the Zap to post into — e.g. Wanderline Leads.
b. In the left sidebar, click Apps.
c. Click the ⋯ (triple-dot) menu, then Find apps.
d. Search Zapier for Google Chat and add it to this space.
Skip this and the space simply won't show up as an option back in Zapier a moment later — there's no error, it just won't be in the list.

Move 2 · Add the action.

7. Once the trigger test succeeds, Zapier drops in an empty step 2 below it, labelled Action — click it.
8. Type Google Chat into the search box and click it. Under Action Event, choose Create Message, then Continue.
9. Connect your Google account for Chat, then Continue.
10. In the step's setup screen, pick the Google Chat connection, then pick Wanderline Leads from the Space dropdown — it's only selectable now because you added the Zapier app to it in the step above.
11. Click into the Message Text field and paste (or type) the message below exactly as written. Then, for each bracketed word, click the small + icon inside the field, and pick the matching field from the list that opens — that's what swaps the bracket for a real orange data pill:

// Google Chat · Message Text — click + and insert a field for every bracketed word
🧭 New lead: [Name]  ([Email])
Trek: [Trek]
"[Summary]"

Move 3 · Test, then publish.

12. Click Test step. A real message lands in the real space, in front of everybody.
13. Click Publish, top-right of the editor — a separate button from Test. Testing is not publishing, exactly like last class's Activate toggle, and it will catch someone in this room within the hour.

The Google Sheets trigger gotcha, and you should demo it breaking: the trigger only sees rows that have data in every column it tracks, needs a proper header row, and ignores rows inserted above existing data. Add a row with a blank column and watch nothing happen. Learners will spend twenty minutes assuming Zapier is broken when the sheet is malformed — thirty seconds now saves that.

Quiz 1 · Farah publishes Zap 1 at 9:00 AM. At 9:02 a new row lands in the leads sheet. She stares at Google Chat. Nothing. At 9:11 the message appears. What happened?

AThe Zap errored on the first attempt and Zapier retried it nine minutes later
BGoogle Chat rate-limited the message and released it once the limit cleared
CNothing is wrong — most Zapier triggers poll on a schedule (typically every 1–15 minutes depending on plan) rather than firing the instant something changes
DThe row was added above existing rows, so it was picked up out of order
Why C? This is the number one "my Zap is broken" that isn't. Most Zapier triggers ask the app "anything new?" on an interval — so a delay of a few minutes is normal operation, not a fault. Some apps do support instant, webhook-based triggers (Zapier labels them Instant in the trigger list); Google Sheets isn't one of them. This matters when you design: never promise a stakeholder "the moment it happens" without checking whether that trigger is instant. A invents a retry that would show in Zap History. B is a real thing but not at one message. D describes a genuine Sheets gotcha — but that causes the row to be missed entirely, not delayed.

Filter by Zapier

A step with no output of its own. It checks a condition against the data flowing through, and either lets the Zap continue or stops it dead right there. It has one exit, not two. Every step below it simply never runs.

🚦
The One Analogy

Last class's IF node was a fork in the road — both directions went somewhere. A Zapier Filter is a bouncer on a door. There's no second room to be sent to; you're either in, or your evening is over. That is a real limitation and also, most of the time, exactly what you wanted — because "do nothing" is the correct response to the overwhelming majority of events.

Live build — insert two steps

Read this before you touch the editor. Zapier has no "insert step 3" menu. Every insertion works the same way: hover the connecting line between two steps that already exist, a small + appears on that line, and whatever you drop there becomes the new step — everything below it renumbers automatically. So every step below always names the two neighbouring steps the + sits between, not a step number, because the number keeps changing as you go.

Insert 1 · the Filter, between Google Sheets and Google Chat.

1. Open your published Zap 1. It shows exactly two steps: 1 · Google Sheets then 2 · Google Chat.
2. Move your mouse onto the connecting line between those two steps — not onto either step, onto the line joining them. A small circular + button fades in on the line.
3. Click that +. A search panel opens. Type Filter by Zapier and click it.
4. It drops in as the new step 2. Google Chat automatically slides down to step 3 — you do not move anything yourself.
5. Set the condition: field Status, dropdown (Text) Exactly matches, value new.
6. Click Test. Read the verdict sentence out loud: "Your Zap would have continued" or "Your Zap would have stopped." Run it once more with a row that should stop, so the room sees the failure state on purpose.

Don't have a Status column? This build assumes the leads sheet already carries a Status column with values like new / replied, written by Kabir's n8n workflow from last class. If you're practising on your own copy of the sheet without that column, add one by hand with a few new rows and a couple of anything-else rows before you build the Filter — otherwise there's nothing real for the condition to check, and Test will just look broken.

Insert 2 · the AI action, between the Filter and Google Chat.

7. Your Zap is now three steps: 1 · Sheets → 2 · Filter → 3 · Google Chat.
8. Move your mouse onto the connecting line between step 2 (the Filter) and step 3 (Google Chat) — one line lower than the + you clicked a moment ago.
9. Click the + that appears there. Type Groq and click it.
10. Choose the action event Send Prompt, then click Continue.
11. Connect it: paste in your own API key from console.groq.com — free to generate, no card required. This is the one credential today that isn't a click-to-authorise, so have it copied and ready before class starts.
12. It drops in as the new step 3. Google Chat slides down again, to step 4 — same automatic renumbering as before.
13. Click into the System Prompt field and paste (or type) the persona and rules below exactly as written. Nothing here needs a data pill — this part is identical for every lead, it's the model's standing instructions, not the enquiry itself:

// Groq · System Prompt
You write for Wanderline, a small Jaipur-based trekking
company. Warm, specific, never salesy. Max 90 words.

Do not invent prices, dates or availability.
End with one question that moves the booking forward.

14. Click into the User Prompt field and paste (or type) the text below exactly as written. Then, for each bracketed word, click the small + icon inside the field and pick the matching field from the list — same move as the Google Chat message a moment ago. This field carries the one thing that's different every single time: the actual enquiry.

// Groq · User Prompt
Write a first reply to this enquiry:
Name: [1. Name]
Trek they asked about: [1. Trek]
Their message: [1. Summary]

That 1. prefix in front of every pill is Zapier's version of last class's $('Node Name') — it names the step the data came from. Step 4 can reach a pill from step 1 just as easily as from step 3. Same idea, and this time you point instead of type.

15. Click into the existing Google Chat step, now sitting at position 4 — don't insert a new one, you're editing the step you already built in Zap 1. Add the AI's reply underneath the lead details, using a pill from step 3.
16. Click Test step, confirm the message looks right, then click Publish again. Editing a published Zap does not republish it — that second Publish click is the one people forget.

Why split the prompt at all? The System Prompt is the model's standing character and rulebook — write it once, and it stays true for the sales-y row, the confused row, and the angry row alike. The User Prompt is the one thing that actually changes per lead. Keeping them apart means you can rewrite Wanderline's whole tone in one field without ever touching how the data flows in — same separation of concerns as last class's system message versus the payload.
Say this out loud — the honest limitation. "Do not invent prices" is an instruction, not a guarantee, and Zapier gives you no schema enforcement here — whatever the model wrote is what goes in the field. That is precisely why this draft lands in Google Chat for a human to read and not in the guest's inbox. Class 10's rule, still holding: automate the rule, keep the judgment. A draft a human approves is a genuinely good product. An unreviewed auto-reply about pricing is a liability with a schedule attached.
Instructor — verify before you teach: confirm Groq's exact app name and the "Send Prompt" action label (and its System Prompt / User Prompt field names) in Zapier's app picker the week you teach — Zapier's AI-provider integrations get renamed and reshuffled more often than the core apps. If Groq isn't listed as a native Zapier app when you check, ChatGPT and Claude both still work identically for this build; the only thing that changes is which console you generate the API key from.

The money detail hiding in that simulator. A filter that stops a Zap costs you nothing — filters aren't billed as tasks. Every action step below it is. So the earlier a filter sits in the stack, the cheaper the Zap. "Put the filter as high as it can go" is a one-line habit that has saved real teams real money, and it is the kind of sentence that makes an interviewer sit up.

🧠 SIM 3 · Map The Prompt — Click, Don't Type

Zapier's real skill isn't prompting, it's mapping. Fill each slot in the User Prompt by clicking the right data pill — shown here under the standing System Prompt rules, so you can see both at once. Pick a wrong one and you'll see exactly what the model sees.

Data pills available from earlier steps
Beginner Note: an unmapped slot doesn't error. It sends the model an empty string, the model writes something plausible around the hole, and you get a fluent, confident, wrong message. Empty is more dangerous than broken — broken tells you.
Intermediate Note: pills are labelled by the step they came from — 1. Name, 3. Reply. When you insert a step in the middle, Zapier renumbers everything, and existing mappings follow correctly. What does not follow: if you delete a step, every pill that pointed at it silently empties. Same failure family as renaming a node in n8n, and just as quiet.
Advanced Note: when a trigger field is sometimes blank, don't leave the slot bare — Zapier lets you type a fallback directly into the field alongside the pill, and Formatter by Zapier → Text → Default Value exists for exactly this. Free step, no task charged, and it turns "Hi ," into "Hi there,".

Quiz 2 · Farah's Zap has a Filter at step 2 that stops about 70% of rows. This month she has used 300 tasks. Kabir suggests moving the Filter to step 4, after the AI step, "so the AI can help decide." What happens to her task usage?

ANothing changes — the same number of rows end in a Google Chat message either way
BIt goes up substantially — every row now pays for the AI step before being thrown away
CIt goes down — filters lower in the stack are evaluated more efficiently
DIt goes up slightly, because the Filter step itself is now billed as a task
Why B? Tasks are counted per successful action step, so every step above the filter runs — and bills — for rows that were always going to be discarded. Moving the filter down means paying an AI call for the 70% you never wanted. A is the trap: the outcome is identical, the cost is not, and outcome-only thinking is how automation bills surprise people. C is invented. D is a common belief but wrong — Filter, Paths, Formatter and Delay are Zapier's own built-in tools and don't count as tasks. Filter as early as the logic allows.
"You now have a four-step Zap that a colleague could read, understand and edit without you in the room. That is a different kind of achievement from building something clever."

Live build — the parts that are actually new

First, one line to the existing AI step — you are not adding a step.

1. Click into step 3, the Groq step you already built in Zap 2.
2. Click into the System Prompt field — not the User Prompt — and add these two lines at the very end, after everything that's already there. It's a standing instruction about the reply's format, not enquiry data, so it belongs with the rules, not with the pills:

Step 4 · Paths by Zapier, inserted after the AI step.

4. Hover the connecting line between step 3 (Groq) and step 4 (Google Chat) and click the + that appears — same move as inserting the Filter and the AI step in Zap 2.
5. Type Paths by Zapier and click it. It drops in as the new step 4 and splits the Zap into Path A and Path B underneath it.
6. In Path A's Rules section: field 3. Reply — (Text) Contains — value BOOKING.
7. In Path B's Rules section: same field, Contains, value CONTENT. Each Rules section behaves exactly like the Filter you already built — it's the same tool, with somewhere to go instead of a dead end.

The thing that surprises everyone, and you should say it before the simulator, not after: Zapier evaluates every path independently. They are not if / else-if. A record whose text contains both words runs both paths — two branches, both sets of actions, both sets of tasks. And a record matching no path just... ends. Silently. Successfully. That combination — accidental doubles and silent drops — is the most common way a Paths Zap misbehaves without ever showing an error.
Before you build inside the paths — delete the old step 5. Inserting Paths pushed your existing Google Chat step down to step 5, sitting below both branches — but a step can't float below a fork; it has to live inside one path or the other. Open its ⋯ menu and click Delete Step now, so you're not tempted to leave it there thinking it still runs. You'll rebuild it in a moment, inside Path A.

Inside Path A · rebuild Google Chat, then add Zapier Tables.

8. Inside Path A's branch, click its +, type Google Chat, choose Create Message again — copy the same message text from Zap 2, and change the Space to Bookings (add the Zapier app to that space first, same one-time step from §1, if you haven't already).
9. Before adding the next step, build the table itself: open Tables in Zapier's left sidebar, click New table, and add the columns date · name · email · trek · category · draft.
10. Back in Path A, click the + below the Google Chat step you just added, type Zapier Tables, choose Create Record, pick the table you just made, and map each column to a pill.

Zapier Tables

A lightweight database that lives inside Zapier. Rows, typed columns, and — the part that matters — it can be a trigger too. New record, or updated record, starts another Zap. That closes a loop the leads spreadsheet never could: your automation now owns its own data instead of borrowing someone's file.

Inside Path B · Notion.

11. Inside Path B's branch, click its +, type Notion, and click it.
12. Before configuring anything in Zapier, go into the target database in Notion itself: open it, click the ⋯ (three dots) in the top-right corner, choose Connections, then add Zapier. Skip this and the database simply won't show up as an option back in Zapier a moment later — same silent gap as the Google Chat space back in §1.
13. Back in Zapier, under Action Event, choose Create Data Source Item, then Continue.
14. Connect Notion, then in the step's configuration: set Data Source to the database you just connected — it's only selectable now because of the Connections step above — leave Template set to No Template, then map the fields of that database to the data coming in from Zapier: title = the trek, body = the guest's question, a Status property set to Idea.

15. Click Test on Path A, then Test on Path B separately — Zapier makes you test each branch on its own. Then click Publish. A branch you never tested is a branch you never built.

Notice what just happened: you connected five different apps across four companies' software — Google Sheets, Google Chat, Groq, Zapier, Notion — into one system, and the only genuinely new concept in this build was the fork. Everything else was "connect, pick, map." That's the learning outcome on the syllabus, and you just did it in about fifteen minutes.

🔀 SIM 4 · Where Does Each Lead Land?

The One Analogy

An IF node was a fork in a road — you go left or you go right, and there is no third option. Zapier Paths are more like a row of pigeonholes with names on them: the sorter checks every box against the letter, and drops a copy into each one that matches. Two names on the envelope means two copies delivered. No name it recognises means the letter quietly goes nowhere, and nobody files a complaint because nothing visibly failed.

Quiz 3 · A lead's AI reply reads: "…happy to help with the booking — and on your content question about altitude…" — the text contains both BOOKING and CONTENT. Path A matches on BOOKING, Path B on CONTENT. What runs?

AOnly Path A — paths are evaluated top to bottom and the first match wins
BBoth paths run in full — Google Chat, a Tables record and a Notion page — and it costs tasks for all three actions
CZapier asks the AI step to break the tie
DThe Zap errors, because a record can't be in two paths at once
Why B? Each path's rules are evaluated independently against the same data — nothing is "used up" by matching. Both fire, both bill. This is the single biggest difference from the IF node you learned last class, and it bites in a specific way: the outputs look correct, so you find out from your task usage or from Divya asking why booking enquiries are appearing on her content board. The fix is to make the categories mutually exclusive at the source — ask the AI for one word on its own final line and match on that, or add a "does not contain" condition to each path. A describes how most people assume it works, and how Paths in some other tools do work — which is exactly why it's worth being sure about this one. C and D don't exist.

§ 4

When It Breaks — Zap History, Held Runs & Replay

⏱ 12 min

All three Zaps work. In a week, one of them won't — and Zapier's failure modes are genuinely different from n8n's, because a Zap can go wrong in four distinct ways and only one of them is called an error.

The Five Words On The Status Column

Open Zap History (left sidebar) and every run carries one of these. Learning to read this column is debugging Zapier — most of the time you never open a step at all.

StatusWhat actually happenedIs it a problem?
SuccessEvery step ran without throwingNot necessarily fine. Same lesson as last class: green means nothing errored, not that it did what you wanted
FilteredA Filter stopped it on purposeNo. This is the system working. A history full of Filtered runs is a well-designed Zap, not a broken one
HeldQueued, not run — usually because you're out of tasks, or the Zap is paused, or you held it yourselfYes, and it's the sneaky one — nothing failed, nothing ran, and no alert email goes out for it
ErrorA step threw. Zapier emails the Zap's ownerYes — but it's the honest failure. It tells you
StoppedHalted before finishing — often a missing required fieldYes. Read the step's input: the field is usually empty, not wrong
The rule that saves the most time: before opening a single step, ask which of these five things happened. "Nothing in Google Chat" has five completely different causes and four of them are diagnosed from the status column alone. Guessing at steps is what beginners do; reading the column is what fast people do.

Two Buttons That Do Not Mean The Same Thing

ButtonWhat it doesBefore you press it
ReplayRe-runs that exact run, from step 1, with the same trigger dataAsk: did any step already succeed? Replay runs the whole thing again — if Google Chat sent and Notion failed, replaying sends the Google Chat message a second time
AutoreplayZapier automatically retries errored runs for you, on paid plansWonderful for a 503. Genuinely dangerous on anything that charges money or messages a customer — same caution as last class's Retry On Fail
And the alert you get for free: Zapier emails the Zap's owner when a Zap errors, and if a Zap keeps erroring it gets turned off and you're told. That's last class's error-workflow safety net, built in — but note who it emails: the account owner. If Farah builds a Zap and leaves the company, those emails go into a mailbox nobody reads. On a team, put shared automations on a shared account.

Quiz 4 · Farah reports "the Zap stopped working on Thursday." You open Zap History: there are runs on Thursday, all marked Held, and none since. No error emails were received. What is the most likely cause?

AThe Google Chat credential expired — held runs are what Zapier shows when an app disconnects

CThe Google Sheets trigger stopped polling because the sheet was edited
DSomeone pressed Replay too many times and Zapier throttled the Zap
Why B? Held is the status that catches people precisely because it is not an error: nothing threw, so nothing emailed anyone, and the Zap looks published and healthy on the dashboard. Running out of tasks is the most common cause (a paused Zap or a manual hold are the others). Note the shape of this failure — it's the same species as last class's "zero executions": the absence of a failure is not the presence of success, and you only see it if you go and look. A would produce Error runs and an email. C isn't a thing. D isn't a thing.

Tasks, Polling & What Actually Costs You Money

⏱ 10 min

This section has no build and it is the most commercially useful ten minutes of the class. n8n's cost was your time. Zapier's cost is a meter that runs while you sleep, and being able to estimate it before you build is a consulting skill.

What Counts, And What Doesn't

Key Takeaways

Start with two steps and publish. One trigger, one action, live. Then grow it. Same law as last class in a different accent: a published two-step Zap beats a beautiful draft.

Testing is not publishing. Zapier's version of the Active toggle, and it catches exactly the same people. Editing a published Zap doesn't republish it either.

Most triggers poll. A few minutes' delay is normal operation, not a fault. Check whether a trigger is Instant before you promise anyone real-time.

A Filter stops; Paths fork. A filter has one exit and no second room. Paths are evaluated independently, so one item can run two branches — or none, silently.

Tasks are the meter, and filters are free. Every app action bills; Zapier's own tools don't. Filter as early as the logic allows — that habit is worth real money.

Success means nothing threw. Held is the dangerous status: nothing ran, nothing failed, nobody was told. Go and look.

You pointed instead of typing — and that was the trade. No expressions to learn, less power when the data gets awkward. Both tools charge you something; know which currency.

The tool question is a maintenance question. Zapier for automations other people need to own. n8n for automations you need to own. Data residency overrules everything.
