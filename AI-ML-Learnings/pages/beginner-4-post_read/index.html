Picture hiring the sharpest analyst in the country. Brilliant with numbers, never wrong about form. But every single morning they wake up having forgotten everything — the shortlist, the workload rules, which stats are confidential, how the committee likes its reports formatted. You spend the first twenty minutes re-briefing them before they can do an hour of real work. That's what a raw AI chat is: a brilliant analyst with total amnesia, every single session. Today's job: give Scout Assist a permanent memory.

§ 2

Tool Demo — Building a Gemini Gem

⏱ 15 min

Let's start with the simplest, fastest tool — free for anyone with a Google account, and the one you'll reach for most often for a single, repeatable job.

What Is a Gem?

A custom version of Gemini that you configure once and reuse forever. Instead of re-pasting your rules and re-uploading your reference files every session, you bake all of it into a dedicated assistant that remembers its job every time you open it. It's completely free.

Here's the threshold question first: when do you build a Gem instead of just prompting?

WHEN TO BUILD A GEM VS. JUST PROMPTING

Think of a task you personally do repeatedly, re-pasting the same context every time — that's your Gem candidate.

Step-by-Step Demo — Building "Net Notes Reviewer"

Step 1 — Open Gem Manager. Go to gemini.google.com. In the left sidebar, click "Gem." Click "+ New Gem."

Step 2 — Name and describe it. Name: Net Notes Reviewer · Description: A strict-but-encouraging reviewer that catches vague scouting notes before they reach the committee.

Step 3 — Write the instructions. This is the heart of the Gem — a permanent system prompt, exactly like Class 3, except it now persists across every conversation with this Gem.

GEM INSTRUCTIONS — "NET NOTES REVIEWER"You are a strict-but-encouraging net session reviewer. For any match or training notes a junior analyst shares, you must: 1. Point out the most important gap or unclear part 2. Explain why that gap matters 3. Rate the notes' clarity on a scale of 1–5 4. Suggest exactly ONE way to improve them Keep responses under 200 words.
Pro tip: If you're not sure how to write detailed instructions, type a simple one-sentence goal and click the magic pencil icon at the bottom of the instructions box. Gemini will auto-expand it into structured instructions you can then edit.

Step 4 — Upload Knowledge Files

Below the instructions field, click "Upload files" and add any reference documents the Gem should draw on. For Net Notes Reviewer, we upload a folder of past gold-standard session reports and the team's stat-naming conventions. Once uploaded, these become permanent context — every conversation with this Gem can reference them.

Preview — Class 5: Think of the Knowledge section as the Gem's long-term memory on disk. The instructions are what the Gem knows how to do. The knowledge files are what it knows about your specific context. What's happening under the hood when the Gem searches those documents is a technique called RAG, Retrieval Augmented Generation — we'll dedicate all of Class 5 to it. When your Gem "reads" a file, it isn't reading it once and remembering it. It's searching, retrieving, and stitching the right passages into every single prompt — five to ten times a session, invisibly. How that search actually works, why it sometimes fails and grabs the wrong passage, and how to fix it when it does — that's the entire first half of Class 5.

Step 5 — Preview and save. Test it in the Preview pane, then hit Save. The Gem appears permanently in the sidebar, ready to reuse.

🧪 SIM 3 · Net Notes Reviewer Console

The instructions above never change. Click through different incoming notes from junior analysts and watch the response keep the exact same shape every time — gap → why it matters → clarity rating → one fix.

None of that structure is hard-coded logic — those rules came from the instructions typed into Step 3. And here's the powerful part: you never have to paste them again. Every review request this Gem receives, from anyone, automatically applies all of it. That's the compounding value of a well-built Gem.

Quiz 1 · You build a Net Notes Reviewer Gem with detailed instructions and upload a folder of past reports. A colleague opens the Gem and starts a brand-new conversation. Which of the following is true?

AThe colleague needs to re-paste the instructions at the start of their conversation
BThe instructions and knowledge files are automatically available in their conversation with the Gem
CThe knowledge files persist, but the instructions must be re-entered for each new conversation
DThe Gem creates a separate copy of the instructions for each user
Why B? This is the core value of a Gem. The instructions and uploaded files are baked into the Gem's configuration — they persist across every conversation, for every colleague who opens that Gem, automatically. Think of it like a pre-briefed analyst: anyone who asks them a question doesn't re-brief them first, they just ask. Option A is the raw-chat experience Gems are designed to eliminate. Option C is wrong — both instructions and files persist together. Option D is wrong — there's no per-user copy; the configuration is shared.

A Different Domain, Same Pattern

One more, and it's got nothing to do with cricket. Here's a Gem built for a personal email inbox:

GEM INSTRUCTIONS — "INBOX TRIAGE"Role: You are an email triage assistant for a busy team ops manager. Context: I receive 50+ emails a day from players, parents, vendors, and the federation. Task: For every email I forward, decide: Reply Now / Can Wait / Delegate to Neha / Ignore. Format: One line only — [DECISION] — [one-sentence reason] Constraints: Never draft the reply itself, only the routing decision. If unsure, default to "Reply Now" — better safe than a missed message.

Notice what just happened — Role, Context, Task, Format, Constraints. Same five slots as RCTFC from Class 2, just saved permanently instead of retyped into every message. Different domain, identical skeleton. Every good Gem in the world uses these five slots — the shortlist Gem, the inbox Gem, all of them.

Gem Danger — When A Gem Is Worse Than Nothing

A Gem with vague instructions — literally just "be helpful" — is worse than a fresh, unconfigured chat. Why? Because it looks configured. Everyone assumes the rules are handled, so nobody double-checks the output — right up until it cites a stat that doesn't exist. If you can't articulate what your Gem does in one sentence, it isn't done yet.

Your Turn — Build a "Trial Bulletin Drafter" Gem

Your exercise: a Gem that always drafts a trial-day bulletin in the exact same five-field format, no matter how messy the incoming message is.

Exercise 2.1 · Build "Trial Bulletin Drafter"

Name: Trial Bulletin Drafter · Description: Turns any messy trial-day message into the exact fixed five-field bulletin, every time.

Instructions (paste below):

You are a Trial Bulletin Assistant. You ONLY reply using this exact fixed format — nothing added, nothing removed: SESSION: DATE: VENUE: KIT REQUIRED: CONFIRM BY: Output ONLY those five fields — no greeting, no sign-off, no extra commentary.

Test it in the Preview pane with: "Fitness testing is this Tuesday morning at the High Performance Centre — bring running shoes, and let Neha know by Monday if you're coming."

What success looks like: exactly the five fields filled in, nothing before or after them. Save your Gem, then test it with a completely different message — the same fixed shape should hold.

Beginner Note: Just paste the instructions exactly. The magic is you'll never have to paste them again.
Advanced Extension [SKIP if short on time]: Upload a folder of real past bulletins as a Knowledge file so the Gem matches your academy's exact house style and phrasing.

Notice the last line: "Output ONLY those five fields." No "Sure, here's the bulletin!" preamble, no sign-off. Precise output constraints are what make an AI tool actually useful inside a workflow instead of adding noise you have to filter out. Always think about what you don't want in the output, not just what you do.

§ 3

Claude Projects — The Department Office

⏱ 15 min

Gems are great for one single, repeatable job. But what if the committee needs a workspace for multiple related tasks that all share the same documents and context?

Imagine Neha's not just doing one thing — some days she's answering player-form questions, some days she's writing workload-risk notes, some days she's drafting strategy summaries for the coach. Different tasks, but they all need the same standing context: the shortlist, the workload policy, the committee's tone. That's exactly what a Claude Project solves. If a Gem is a specialist with your file permanently on their desk, a Claude Project is a dedicated office room — filing cabinet full of reference material, whiteboard with your standing rules, and every conversation held in that room automatically inherits all of it, regardless of which task it is.

Not just Claude: This pattern isn't a Claude-only idea. Perplexity's "Spaces" feature is a Claude Project by another name — same shared instructions, same shared documents, different logo. And Anthropic's own engineers ship an internal Project for every team — it's literally how their own product managers stay organised. Once you've built one, you'll recognise this exact mechanic everywhere you look.
HOW A PROJECT DIFFERS FROM A REGULAR CONVERSATION

Three things change inside a Project:

  1. Project Knowledge — uploaded documents that persist across every conversation in the project
  2. Project Instructions — a permanent system prompt for every conversation
  3. Grouped conversations — all chats inside the project stay organised together
🏢
Analogy · Substitute Scout vs. Permanent Staff

A regular AI conversation is like calling in a substitute scout who's never met the team, parachuted in for one match — you brief them from scratch. A Claude Project is a permanent member of staff who already knows the shortlist, the workload rules, and the committee's tone, no matter which task lands on their desk that day.

Exercise 3.1 · Build a Claude Project — "Selection Committee Ops"

Step 1 — Open claude.ai → Projects → "Create a Project." Name it Selection Committee Ops.

Step 2 — Find "Set project instructions" and paste:

CLAUDE PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS — "SELECTION COMMITTEE OPS"You are Scout Assist, the scouting assistant for Team India's selection committee. ## Standing Rules - Always cite the match or date a stat came from - Always mention if a player has a workload flag — without naming the specific injury - Only reference players on the verified shortlist — never invent a stat, player, or ranking - Tone: formal and precise, the way a scout briefs the committee, not a casual chat ## Default Behaviour - Player queries: cite the source, flag workload risk, end by asking if they want the full report - Roster or strategy questions: think about balance across the XI, explain trade-offs - Unclear requests: ask a clarifying question before guessing ## Safety Rules - Never share a player's private medical details — only a general workload flag - Never speculate about a player's private life

Step 3 — Upload Project Knowledge

Create a short reference file, workload_flag_policy.md, with these rules, and upload it alongside the verified shortlist:

- A workload flag means a player has crossed their monitored bowling/fielding load this week - Never state the specific injury or medical detail — only that a flag exists - Always recommend checking with the physio team before selecting a flagged player for back-to-back matches - Flags reset weekly unless the physio team extends them

🏢 SIM 4 · Two Conversations, One Shared Doctrine

Two different selectors start two brand-new, unrelated conversations inside "Selection Committee Ops." Watch how both inherit the exact same rules without either of them typing a single one.

Look carefully at what happened in those two conversations. Neither selector typed "cite the source" or "don't name the injury." The project instructions silently injected all of it — like a shared team playbook stapled inside every notebook everyone in the department carries. Nobody rereads the playbook each week; it's just already applied.

Beginner Note: You're just filling in text boxes and uploading files. No code to run.
Intermediate Note: Notice how different this is from a Gem — one project, multiple conversation types, shared knowledge across all of them.
Advanced Extension [SKIP if short on time]: Try uploading a real past scouting report and ask Claude to draft a one-page summary in the same house style — the project instructions will make sure it matches your conventions.

Project Danger — More Files Isn't More Context

Uploading every document you own doesn't make a Project smarter — it bloats it. Every extra file is more material the assistant has to search through and weigh against everything else, and the model gets less relevant on every single retrieval, not more. Five focused, current files beat fifty random ones every time. Curate the knowledge base like you'd curate a briefing pack, not a dumping ground.

Quiz 2 · A committee sets up "Selection Committee Ops" as a Claude Project. They upload the verified shortlist and the workload policy, and set project instructions defining tone and citation rules. A selector starts a brand-new conversation and asks: "Any spin options for a turning pitch?" What will the assistant use to generate its answer?

AOnly the selector's message — the assistant does not automatically use project context
BThe project instructions only — uploaded documents are not used unless explicitly referenced
CBoth the project instructions AND the uploaded shortlist and workload policy
DThe project instructions only on the first conversation; later conversations start fresh
Why C? Every conversation inside the project automatically receives two things: the project instructions (the permanent system prompt) and access to the project knowledge (the uploaded documents). Claude draws on both when generating a response — without the selector needing to reference or attach anything manually. That's exactly why Projects are powerful for team workflows: anyone who opens a conversation inside the project immediately has the full shared context, with zero setup on their end.

Google AI Studio — The Analytics Lab

⏱ 20 min

Gems and Claude Projects are configured through friendly UIs that abstract away the mechanics. Google AI Studio is different — it gives you the same models, but with the hood open.

🚗
Analogy · Transmission Types

Gemini chat = automatic transmission — you type, it goes. A Gem = automatic with cruise control and saved presets. AI Studio = manual transmission — you control the gears yourself.

THE THREE KEY FEATURES · SYSTEM INSTRUCTIONS, MODEL PARAMETERS, MULTI-TURN TESTING

Model Parameters — What The Dials Do

TEMPERATURE, TOP-P, TOP-K, MAX TOKENS

Let's talk about the most important dial: temperature. Here's the intuition. Imagine you ask ten different people to name a colour. At low temperature — high certainty — almost everyone says "blue" or "red." Predictable, reliable, a little boring. At high temperature — high randomness — you get turquoise, chartreuse, burnt sienna, the colour of a sunset over the stadium. Creative, surprising, occasionally unusable. Set it low for tasks that need precision — a scouting stat sheet. Set it higher for tasks that benefit from flair — a matchday social post.

Beginner Note: You don't need to memorise the numbers. The key insight: temperature = how creative vs. predictable the AI is.

🌡️ SIM 5 · Temperature Dial

Same system instructions, same request — "Write a short congratulations message for Arjun Rathi, who just took a hat-trick on debut." Drag the dial and watch what changes.

0.1
0.1 · Safe0.7 · Warmer1.2 · Wild Card

Before you run all three yourself in AI Studio — predict: will 1.2 give a better or worse message than 0.1?

Try running the 0.1 version twice — you'll get almost the same message both times. Now run the 1.2 version twice — you'll probably get two completely different messages. That's not "good vs. bad," it's "consistent vs. varied." A stat-extraction bot? You want 0.1. A brainstorming buddy? 0.7. A creative caption generator? 1.0 and up — surprise me.

AI Studio Danger — The Dial Doesn't Override The Task

Setting temperature to 0.0 and expecting variety is the classic AI Studio mistake — you'll get the same safe output every time and wonder where the creativity went. The opposite mistake is just as common: setting it to 1.5 and expecting clean, structured output — like reliable JSON-style fields — and getting garbled nonsense instead. The dial doesn't override the task, it amplifies the model's existing tendencies. A task that's naturally rigid stays rigid at any temperature; a task that's naturally loose gets looser.

Exercise 4.1 · Multi-Turn Prompt Testing

AI Studio lets you test multi-turn conversations — a back-and-forth where you check whether the AI remembers what you said earlier. Keep the same system instructions, set temperature to 0.5.

1 Turn 1 — Set The Scene

This is about context retention — can the AI keep track of a growing conversation? In a well-configured session, the final answer should include everything from turn 1 and turn 2 in one place, without repeating the budget or location. Quiz 3 · You're building a tool that pulls structured stats out of messy analyst voice notes and always outputs the same fixed set of fields. Which temperature setting is most appropriate, and why?

A1.2 — high creativity ensures the model finds every relevant detail
B0.1 — low temperature gives consistent, predictable structured outputs
C0.7 — a balanced setting works well for every task
D0.5 — a middle setting prevents the output from being too rigid or too random
Why B? When your task is extracting structured data — parsing, classifying, filling fixed fields — you want the exact same reliable output every time. High temperature introduces randomness, which is the last thing you want when populating a stat sheet. Think of it like a scoreboard: you don't want it showing a different total each time you add up the same runs. Option A is wrong — creativity is the enemy of consistency here. Option C is a general default, not optimal for this job. Option D is the tricky one — it sounds reasonable, but even moderate temperature introduces enough variance to break structured consistency, and that small unpredictability is exactly what causes downstream formatting to break.

Wrap-Up — The Decision Framework

⏱ 10 min
THE DECISION FRAMEWORK · ONE JOB OR MANY? SHARED WITH A TEAM? TUNING MODEL BEHAVIOUR?

Here's the logic, crystallised. If you have a single, repeatable task — build a Gem. Free, 5 minutes, saves hours over time. If you have a whole domain — multiple task types sharing the same knowledge — build a Claude Project, your persistent working environment. If you need to understand how the model behaves, or tune its dials before building it into something bigger — use AI Studio, your lab. Most professional AI users don't pick one and stick to it. They use all three for different jobs — the same way a senior analyst uses different tools for different stages of the work.

Key Takeaways

  1. The amnesia problem is solved. Gems, Projects, and AI Studio all give persistent context — set up once, use forever.
